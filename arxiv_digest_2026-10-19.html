#1
Timelike entanglement entropy with gravitational anomaly from complexified geometry
Minwoo Lim, Himanshu Parihar, Junggi Yoon 2610.23514v1 [PDF] Score 9
AdS/CFTCFTEntanglement & Geometryhep-th
We investigate the timelike entanglement entropy (TEE) in a two-dimensional conformal field theory with a gravitational anomaly, $c_L\neq c_R$, dual to topologically massive gravity in AdS$_3$. Holographically, the TEE is determined by the on-shell action of a massive spinning particle on a single complex geodesic anchored at the endpoints of the timelike interval. This action consists of the complex geodesic length and the twist of a parallel-transported normal frame. We carry out the computation in Poincaré AdS$_3$, non-extremal and extremal rotating BTZ backgrounds, and provide an equivalent Chern-Simons derivation in which the bulk connection stays real while only the saddle of the Wilson-line probe becomes complex. In each case, the holographic result matches the corresponding TEE in dual field theory. The real part of the TEE is fixed unambiguously whereas its imaginary part depends on the boundary $iε$ prescription and the orientation of the interval. In the bulk, the dependence on orientation reflects an ambiguity in the choice of framing. Along the complex geodesic, the residual frame group becomes $SO(2,\mathbb C)\simeq\mathbb C^*$, so the endpoint conditions determine the frame twist only up to integer multiples of $2πi$. The difference between the two orientations, $\frac{c_L-c_R}{6}\,iπ$, is fixed entirely by the gravitational anomaly and persists for the Rényi pseudo-entropies.
#2
Principle of no paradoxical Universe
Ngo Phuc Duc Loc 2610.23606v1 [PDF] Score 6
Quantum Gravityhep-phastro-ph.COgr-qc
In the standard $Λ\rm CDM$ model, the Universe will keep expanding forever and its lifetime is infinite. Therefore, even the heaviest supermassive black hole will eventually evaporate and the universal occurrence of the black hole information paradox is unavoidable. However, the recent DESI results seem to indicate that dark energy is dynamical and, more importantly, it is possible that the Universe has a finite lifetime and will experience a ``Big Crunch" in some billion years from now. In light of this, the lightest confirmed black hole with mass of order one solar mass may not have a chance to evaporate before the Universe ends. Inspired by these observations, we thus raise an intriguing conjecture: Does the Universe choose a configuration such that the ``in-principle" black hole information paradox can never actually happen in reality? We derive a lower bound on black hole mass as a function of the Universe's lifetime such that the information paradox cannot be realized in nature and some resulting predictions are made. Some profound implications for other related big ideas in physics are briefly discussed.

Spinors, Doppelgängers and the Non-Perturbative Classical Double Copy
Kymani Armstrong-Williams 2610.23384v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFThep-thgr-qchep-ph
We develop a geometric and spinorial formulation of the non-perturbative classical double copy between the Corrigan--Fairlie--'t Hooft--Wilczek sector of complexified SU(2) Yang--Mills theory and four-dimensional conformally flat gravity. We identify the gauge potential with the positive-chiral spin connection of the conformally flat metric, establishing an exact relation between the Yang--Mills field strength and the chiral projection of the Riemann tensor. This provides a four-dimensional realisation of the doppelgänger viewpoint, in which gauge and gravitational fields arise from different interpretations of common connection data. Within this sector, the sourceless Yang--Mills equations require constant scalar curvature, explaining the shared nonlinear scalar equation and extending the gravitational correspondence from traceless matter to matter with constant energy--momentum trace. By representing adjoint colour indices as symmetric pairs of spinor indices, we derive exact relations mapping the gauge field-strength spinors to the scalar-curvature and trace-free Ricci components of the gravitational curvature. These relations retain nontrivial curvature information despite the vanishing Weyl tensor. We illustrate the construction using anti-de Sitter and de Sitter spacetimes, together with $\mathrm{AdS}_{3}\times\mathbb{R}$ supported by a scalar field, demonstrating the extension to matter with nonzero constant trace

Scalar Casimir Effect from Bilocal Correlations in a Wormhole Foam
Celio R. Muniz, M. C. Araújo, Edson Otoniel et al. 2610.23369v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometryhep-th
We determine how spatially bilocal correlations induced by a dilute ensemble of virtual Euclidean wormholes modify the scalar Casimir interaction between parallel Dirichlet plates. From the leading monopole action, we isolate an equal-Euclidean-time sector and construct a point-throat, first-order matched spectator model with fixed foam statistics. Global two-plate inclusion-exclusion over all mouth-pair center positions yields a continuous fixed-separation kernel free of the logarithmic image structures produced by a restricted translation sector. The kernel is quadratically suppressed at coincidence, changes sign near 0.695 plate spacings, and tends to a positive large-separation plateau. Averaging over truncated-Gaussian, log-normal, and Gamma distributions with a common relative width yields closely spaced energy curves but a finite shape dependence. Differentiating the dimensional energy at fixed ensemble parameters gives a leading-order pressure threshold of 0.05 in the large-separation limit. In the truncated-Gaussian scan extending to twelve plate spacings, an amplitude of 0.06 yields a crossover near a separation ratio of 3.17, whereas amplitudes of 0.02 and 0.04 remain attractive and 0.05 is critical. The scalar cavity therefore provides a scale-selective probe of spatial bilocality whose leading boundary response can change sign within the declared model.

Consistency Checks for Λ positive Quantum Cosmology
S. P. Miao, N. C. Tsamis, R. P. Woodard 2610.23168v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravitygr-qchep-phhep-th
In this paper we improve and further analyze the recently derived quantum gravity equations which apply in accelerating cosmological spacetimes and whose solutions re-sum leading logarithms from all orders of perturbation theory. We show the consistency of these upgraded equations against 1-loop non-trivial exact perturbative computations. The upgraded equations retain their remarkably simple form.

Near-Extremal Black-Hole Formation Observed from Null Infinity
Thomas Mädler, Radouane Gannouji, Steven L. Liebling et al. 2610.23486v1 [PDF] Score 3
Celestial Holographygr-qc
We identify near-extremal black-hole formation directly from observables at future null infinity. Fully nonlinear evolutions of the spherically symmetric Einstein--Maxwell--charged scalar system in 3+1 dimensions form black holes with charge-to-mass ratios up to 0.993 from regular, horizon-free, superextremal initial data. Quasinormal frequencies and redshift decay independently recover the near-extremal charge-to-mass ratio. Critical mass scaling is recovered at null infinity and charge scaling at the apparent horizon. The charged scalar Newman--Penrose quantity obeys an asymptotic balance law that replaces the conservation law of the neutral theory.
#7
Memory effects in the homogenization of a viscous fluid containing elastic particles
J. Casado-Díaz, A. R. Hormigo-Molina 2610.23819v1 [PDF] Score 3
Asymptotic Symmetriesmath.APmath-ph
This work deals with a fluid-structure interaction problem involving an incompressible viscous fluid and a periodic array of tiny elastic inclusions in a three-dimensional domain. We focus on a critical regime where the volume fraction of the inclusions tends to zero, but their density and stiffness are scaled so that they still influence the macroscopic behavior. The asymptotic regime depends on the parameter $γ=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}r_\varepsilon/\varepsilon^3\in [0,+\infty]$, where $\varepsilon$ is the period and $r_\varepsilon$ the inclusion size. For $γ=0$ the solid and fluid equations uncouple in the limit. For $γ\in (0,+\infty]$, we show that the macroscopic fluid velocity satisfies a Stokes system with memory: the standard Stokes equations are augmented by convolution-in-time terms arising from the elastic vibrations of the inclusions. The existence of the convolution kernels is established for both $γ\in(0,+\infty)$ and $γ=\infty$ using semigroup theory. For $γ=\infty$ the microscopic problem reduces to a purely conservative elastodynamic equation and the kernels can be computed explicitly via spectral decomposition. They are oscillatory and almost-periodic in time, yielding a persistent, non-decaying memory. For $γ\in(0,+\infty)$ the microscopic elastodynamic equation is coupled to a local Stokes problem, whose viscous dissipation yields memory kernels that decay to zero at infinity.
