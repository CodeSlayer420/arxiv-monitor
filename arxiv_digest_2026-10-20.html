Higher-Dimensional Test of the Holographic Dual of Entanglement Cohomology
R. ~C. ~Rashkov 2610.25969v1 [PDF] Score 16
AdS/CFTCFTEntanglement & Geometryhep-th
We extend the holographic test of entanglement cohomology from $d=2$ to higher-dimensional conformal field theories. For a $d$-dimensional CFT with a spherical entangling region $A$, the vacuum modular Hamiltonian is known explicitly, and the boundary relative entropy of a primary-operator perturbation can be computed via conformal Ward identities. On the bulk side, the perturbation is dual to a scalar field in AdS$_{d+1}$, and the bulk canonical energy is computed using the JLMS relation. We carry out the computation explicitly for $d=3$ and $d=4$ and find that the boundary and bulk results agree at first order in the perturbation, with identical coefficients $κ_d Δ^2 / C_T$, where $κ_3 = π^2/12$ and $κ_4 = 3π^3/16$. The result extends the quantitative verification of the holographic dual of entanglement cohomology beyond the $d=2$ case treated in the companion paper and confirms that the modular Hamiltonian perturbation is the correct holographic image of the first entanglement cohomology class. We discuss the role of the conformal anomaly, the shape of the entanglement wedge, and an outlook on TsT-deformed backgrounds. \keywords{Entanglement entropy \and Cyclic cohomology \and von Neumann algebras \and Holography \and Modular Hamiltonian}

Subsystem quantum Rényi-Bhattacharyya distance in 2D CFTs and its holographic applications
Yihao Wang, Jiaju Zhang 2610.25685v1 [PDF] Score 10
AdS/CFTCFTEntanglement & GeometryQuantum Gravityhep-thcond-mat.stat-mechgr-qc
We develop a systematic method to compute the subsystem fidelity and Rényi fidelity in two-dimensional conformal field theories (CFTs) from the operator product expansion of twist operators, and apply it to the subsystem quantum Bhattacharyya distance (BD) and Rényi-Bhattacharyya distance (RBD). We validate the method against exact results in the free massless fermion and boson theories, and then use it to compute the BD and RBD in holographic CFTs between two heavy primary states, between two thermal states, and between a heavy primary state and a thermal state. When the two states differ in their classical, order-$c$ data, the distances are of order $c$, and the states are distinguishable already at the level of classical gravity; when the leading data coincide, the distances are of order $1/c$ at every order of the short interval expansion, so that the distinguishability becomes a perturbative quantum gravity effect. For a heavy primary state and its thermal counterpart, the leading contribution to the BD appears at order $\ell^8$ and is of order $c^0$, whereas the RBD is generically of order $c$, in line with the earlier finding that a heavy primary state and a thermal state share the same order-$c$ entanglement entropy but have different order-$c$ Rényi entropies in the short interval expansion.

Mechanical angular momentum loss at $\mathcal{O}(G^4)$ from static and radiative gravitons
Lara Bohnenblust, Carl Jordan Eriksen, Gustav Uhre Jakobsen et al. 2610.26822v1 [PDF] Score 5
Asymptotic Symmetrieshep-thgr-qc
We determine the loss of mechanical angular momentum $L^μ_{\rm loss}$ of two gravitationally scattering bodies at $\mathcal{O}(G^4)$ using worldline quantum field theory (WQFT). The gravitational memory effect, a phenomenon of the early- and late-time behaviour of the gravitational field, leads to static contributions to $L^μ_{\rm loss}$. We illuminate how these arise: the linear memory contributes through so-called static correlators, while the nonlinear memory vanishes at this order due to symmetry considerations. The computation of the remaining, dynamical part resembles that of the $\mathcal{O}(G^4)$ impulse. We generate its integrand using a reformulation of the WQFT Feynman rules that conserves four-momentum at every vertex and plainly exhibits the classical loops. The three-loop integrals are reduced to master integrals, whose differential equations and boundary matching in the post-Newtonian limit we describe in some detail. In contrast to conservative effects at this order, only polylogarithms appear. While individual contributions to the dynamical part are divergent, these cancel in $L^μ_{\rm loss}$. The quadratic-in-radiation part of our result reproduces earlier results, while the complete result agrees with existing post-Newtonian results.

U(1) Current Diffusion in Gauss-Bonnet Holography
A. Naveena Kumara, Shreyas Punacha, Kartheek Hegde 2610.26515v1 [PDF] Score 5
AdS/CFThep-thgr-qc
We study holographic $U(1)$ charge transport using a probe Maxwell field in a five-dimensional Gauss-Bonnet-AdS black-brane background. Within the all-order linearised fluid/gravity framework, we derive the off-shell constitutive relation and determine the diffusion function and electric and magnetic conductivities analytically in the hydrodynamic regime and numerically at generic frequency and momentum. To first order in the Gauss-Bonnet coupling, the leading diffusion constant remains unchanged in our normalization, while its frequency dependence and the electric conductivity receive nontrivial corrections. The deformation also generates a nonvanishing leading magnetic conductivity. Beyond the hydrodynamic regime, it modifies the transport profiles while preserving the damped behaviour of the diffusion function. The corresponding memory kernel vanishes at negative times, supporting a retarded constitutive response.

Boundary preparation of strong Mpemba states in holography
Yu Shi, Xinwu Liu, Liping Li et al. 2610.26218v1 [PDF] Score 4
AdS/CFThep-th
Finite boundary drives ending at the same charge density can select different relaxation channels. We demonstrate this in a probe Abelian--Higgs model on a fixed AdS black brane with a stable normal endpoint. Charge exchange during preparation modifies scalar propagation and source coupling. Density history and radial Maxwell feedback during and after the drive leave a cubic slow amplitude even after linear slow-mode cancellation. For the tested protocols at $ρ_f=3$, the density-history term interferes constructively with the net radial feedback; its modulus is 85--89\% of that of the total cubic coefficient. Calculated from the low-order equations alone, this total coefficient predicts the compensating pulse. Full nonlinear evolution confirms the reduction from $O(ε^3)$ to $O(ε^5)$ in the source amplitude $ε$, with approximately 600-fold suppression for the representative protocol at $ε=0.05$. Compensation extends the window of fast-channel relaxation without changing the final quasinormal-mode spectrum; early distance crossing already arises from linear interference. Fully tuned two- and three-pulse preparations suppress one and two slow modes, respectively, within numerical resolution. Local analysis establishes finite-pulse reachability of strong Mpemba states with vanishing selected asymptotic slow-mode coefficients. Charge-neutral controls retain a total-time advantage from a common input when the full subsequent control duration is included.

Black hole free energy landscapes from constrained instantons
Shan-Ping Wu, Jia-Zhou Liu, Yu-Xiao Liu et al. 2610.26094v1 [PDF] Score 4
AdS/CFTGeometric Gravitygr-qchep-th
We apply gravitational path integral factorization to provide a semiclassical interpretation of generalized black hole free energy landscapes at fixed external temperature and angular velocities. Boundary integral transforms express the formal partition function as an integral over constrained sectors labeled by complete local partial-Neumann data on an auxiliary hypersurface. Independent variation establishes paired regular subregion solutions as constrained instantons, with Lagrange multipliers supporting lapse and tangential shift jumps that accommodate thermal and rotational mismatches. For selected nonextremal stationary families with an asymptotic boundary, the constrained saddle action takes the form $I_{\rm constr}=β\mathcal F_{\rm gen}=β(E_{\mathcal B}-Ω_{\mathcal B}^{I}J_{\mathcal B,I})-S$, with $β=T^{-1}$. Here $E_{\mathcal B}$ and $J_{\mathcal B,I}$ are renormalized Hamiltonian charges and $S$ is the black hole entropy, whose contribution to the action comes from the regular horizon cap. Interior stationary points of the landscape correspond to smooth equilibrium saddles when the retained variations independently span the entropy and angular momentum directions. We extend the construction to local, parity-even $f(\mathrm{Riemann})$ theories without curvature derivatives by retaining the complete auxiliary interface data. The same thermodynamic form follows with Wald entropy, without a small-deficit expansion. Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes illustrate the Einstein construction, while five-dimensional static AdS black holes in Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet gravity and the rotating BTZ branch of new massive gravity provide explicit checks of the higher-curvature extension.

Giorgos Anastasiou, Ignacio J. Araya, Avijit Das et al. 2610.24698v1 [PDF] Score 4
AdS/CFTCFThep-thgr-qc
We formulate the extremization problem for codimension-two hypersurfaces in de Sitter space in holomorphic coordinates, with real anchoring conditions at future infinity. The resulting equation ($K^I=0$) describes complex bulk realizations extending the usual piecewise Hartle--Hawking construction. We compute holographic pseudoentropy for harmonic deformations of spherical regions of arbitrary size and dimension, confirming its universal expansion through second order in the deformation parameter. The result is independent of the integration contour within each homology class on the complexified extremal surface. Inequivalent classes, labeled by an integer associated with a discrete global-coordinate isometry, differ by a sign of the universal term in odd boundary dimensions and by real finite shifts in even dimensions. For conical defects in dS$_3$ parametrized by a Schwarzschild-dS mass, we also verify the first law of pseudoentropy and the persistence of contour degeneracy within each homology class.

Interferometric trilemma for ultraviolet-softened gravity
Hiroki Matsui 2610.25994v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravitygr-qchep-th
Ultraviolet-softened extensions of general relativity can weaken gravity at short distances, whereas a positive Källén--Lehmann spectral density forbids gravity from falling below Newtonian strength. Which property of the mediator must such a weakening sacrifice? For Lorentz-invariant Gaussian mediators coupled linearly to the conserved stress tensor, we formulate three independent interferometric conditions that jointly imply this bound: visibility is never amplified, decoherence is fixed by the energy radiated by the difference source, and no interferometer responds to a later one. At tree level, the three ghost prescriptions of quadratic gravity each violate exactly one: negative-norm ghosts amplify coherence, negative-energy ghosts decohere without positive energy transfer from the difference source, and a time-symmetric fakeon response produces advanced fringe shifts. Any universal short-distance weakening of gravity therefore certifies that its mediator violates one of these conditions, and the observables proposed to certify quantum gravity become consistency tests of its ultraviolet completion.

The long-range Ising CFT: perturbative and nonperturbative approaches
Zechuan Zheng 2610.25940v1 [PDF] Score 3
CFThep-thcond-mat.stat-mech
The long-range Ising model is the prototype of criticality with power-law interactions. In each dimension its interacting critical points form a one-parameter family of nonlocal conformal field theories, weakly coupled at both ends but not in between. We study this family in one, two and three dimensions from several independent directions. We extend the expansion about the generalized free field from three to eight loops and expand about the short-range end. We show that Hamiltonian truncation applies to this nonlocal theory once a truncation artifact is corrected. Where they can be compared, the resummed expansions, Hamiltonian truncation, the numerical renormalization group and Monte Carlo simulations agree on the dimension of the leading $\mathbb{Z}_2$-even scalar at the sub-percent level, as universality requires.

Optical constraints on rotating logarithmic- and exponential-entropy wormholes from strong-field lensing

Entanglement & Geometrygr-qc
The longstanding issue of whether horizonless compact objects can mimic black holes at horizon scales is addressed, a problem that persists because the shadow diameter alone is degenerate across a wide class of geometries. For slowly rotating traversable wormholes, we precisely formulate the optical appearance as a set of observables derived from equatorial null geodesics, yielding necessary and sufficient conditions for a multi-parameter fingerprint. Using a new lapse function that interpolates between exponential and rational redshift profiles, we obtain the photon-sphere condition, the strong-deflection lensing coefficients, and the Lyapunov exponent in closed form. The equations yield two families of wormhole solutions dependent on logarithmic and exponential entropy parameters. We compare the resulting quantum-modified spacetimes with Kerr and four regular black holes. The shadow diameter is shown to be tunable to within $5\%$ to $11\%$ of Kerr for all models considered, so that $|R_{\rm sh}^{\rm WH}/R_{\rm sh}^{\rm Kerr}-1| \lesssim 0.11$ and the shadow alone cannot separate the models. The degeneracy is lifted when the rotational asymmetry, lensing coefficients, Lyapunov exponent, and orbital frequency are combined: for the $5$-component fingerprint $\mathbf{F}=(\Arot,\abar,\bbar, \Lya,\Oph)$, the fractional deviation $Δ_i=(F_i^{\rm WH}-F_i^{\rm Kerr})/ F_i^{\rm Kerr}$ satisfies $|Δ_i|\gtrsim 0.05$ for every component, with the largest departures in the second lensing coefficient and the Lyapunov exponent. The combined discrimination signal reaches a signal-to-noise ratio of about $3.8$ to $3.9$ against Kerr and about $3.7$ against the Kazakov--Solodukhin benchmark, which is sufficient to break the shadow-diameter degeneracy at the $10\%$ measurement level.

De Finetti Structures in Networks of Quantum Systems
Gavin Hull, Marco Merkli 2610.25280v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometrymath-phquant-ph
Consider $N$ identical quantum systems at the vertices of a graph, interacting via pairwise edge couplings. We designate $K$ vertices as a system of interest $\mathrm S$ and the remaining $N-K$ as a reservoir $\mathrm R$. Under generic connectivity assumptions between $\mathrm S$ and $\mathrm R$, we show that for $N\to\infty$, the reduced state of $\mathrm S$ converges to a convex combination of symmetric product states of $K$ single-vertex density matrices -- called a de Finetti representation. Unlike in standard derivations for symmetric (exchangeable) systems, we establish de Finetti representations for quantum networks, which are inherently non-exchangeable. We construct the representations explicitly and quantify the convergence rate. We show that Erdős--Rényi random graphs exhibit the de Finetti structure: as the reservoir grows, the reduced state of any fixed finite subgraph converges almost surely to an explicit deterministic product state. As applications, we establish a general area law for the entanglement entropy of $\mathrm S$, analyze mean-field and fluctuation scalings of the $\mathrm S$-$\mathrm R$ interaction, and illustrate our results on qubit networks. We also derive quantitative convergence estimates for information-theoretic quantities, including von Neumann entropy, mutual information, and entanglement within $\mathrm S$ and between $\mathrm S$ and $\mathrm R$.
