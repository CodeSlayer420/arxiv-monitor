Theory and Cosmology of Biverse Volume-Normalized Gravity
Siqing Yu 2610.35384v1 [PDF] Score 13
AdS/CFTCFTQuantum GravityString Theoryhep-thgr-qc
This dissertation expounds the biverse volume-normalized gravity, a novel theoretical framework of classical and quantum gravity that provides an unusual solution to the cosmological constant problem, the 60-degree-of-magnitude discrepancy between the value of the vacuum energy density in our universe predicted by particle physics and that implied by cosmological data. In the literature, there exist two interesting ideas: 1) to use the "volume-normalized" action functional, the usual action of the particle Standard Model and general relativity but divided by the spacetime volume, first proposed by Tseytlin; 2) to generate the vacuum energy density dynamically by assigning a ghost-free massive potential to the graviton. However, each has a problem: Tseytlin's attempt suffers from diverging quantum loops and the massive gravity theory has a second metric tensor with questionable origin and the quantum strong coupling problem. Inspired by these two ideas, as well as the AdS/CFT correspondence in string theory, the biverse volume-normalized gravity extends Tseytlin's idea by placing our (3+1)-dimensional universe on the boundary of a (4+1)-dimensional bulk space. This new adaptation controls the large quantum gravity loops in the limit of vanishing ratio of the spacetime volume of our universe to that of the other universe. The ghost-free massive gravity can then be seamlessly incorporated, not only that its otherwise nebulous second metric tensor appears naturally as the pullback of the bulk metric on the boundary, but its strong coupling problem also solved. The biverse volume-normalized gravity is also consistent with all tested models of particle cosmology and the evolution of the universe, and nontrivially predicts that the tensor-to-scalar ratio of our universe is exactly zero.

On 2-loop AdS$_2$ superstring partition function in AdS$_5 \times S^5$ and AdS$_4\times CP^3$
Arkady A. Tseytlin 2610.37096v1 [PDF] Score 7
AdS/CFTString Theoryhep-th
We revisit the 2-loop computation of the partition function of the GS superstring expanded near the AdS$_2$ surface in AdS$_5\times S^5$ and AdS$_4\times {\rm CP}^3$, dual to $\frac{1}{2}$ BPS circular Wilson loop in ${\cal N}=4$ SYM and ABJM theories. In arXiv:2601.08809 it was done in AdS$_5\times S^5$ using dimensional regularization and a UV pole was found. Since dimensional regularization is problematic for the GS string, here we use a heat-kernel cutoff instead. Then the contribution of the local path integral measure, proportional to $δ(0)$, can no longer be ignored. We show that it cancels the $δ(0)$ terms coming from the quartic interactions in the action, leaving the square of the difference ${\rm Str}\,E$ between the regularized numbers of bosonic and fermionic modes. The result is thus UV finite, the pole of arXiv:2601.08809 being an artefact of dimensional regularization. If all fields are regularized with the same smearing kernel, ${\rm Str}\,E=0$ and the 2-loop coefficient $f_2$ vanishes, in tension with $f_2=\frac{3}{32π^2}$ in SYM theory. In the AdS$_4\times{\rm CP}^3$ case the same cancellation leaves a finite term proportional to the square of the coincident value of the massless 2d fermion Green's function, giving, ironically, $f_2=\frac{3}{32π^2}$ instead of $f_2=0$ required by localization. This term is the level-zero (string) part of the result of arXiv:2609.14497 for the AdS$_2\times S^1$ M2 brane in AdS$_4\times S^7/\mathbb Z_k$, where it is cancelled by the higher $S^1$ modes, which also remove a finite mass shift of the massless fermion in conflict with the AdS$_2$ supersymmetry. This suggests that the type II string path integral measure should contain an extra contribution, supplied in the IIA case by the parent M2 brane; in the AdS$_5\times S^5$ case it is still to be identified and may resolve the remaining 2-loop mismatch with localization.

Axionic Quintessence in Heterotic String Theory
David Andriot, Margherita Putti, Leo Veissiere 2610.36064v1 [PDF] Score 7
String Theoryhep-thastro-ph.COgr-qc
We investigate axionic quintessence in heterotic string compactifications. Starting from the ten-dimensional theory, we derive the four-dimensional axion effective action, including gauge couplings and non-perturbative potentials, and identify the curvature terms that can correct it. We examine the geometric requirements for ultralight axions generated by exponentially suppressed worldsheet instantons, finding significant restrictions through an exploratory scan of toric Calabi-Yau hypersurfaces. In an illustrative K3-fibred setup, the minimal benchmark also encounters a tension with the positivity of the one-loop corrected gauge kinetic functions. Aligned worldsheet-instanton contributions provide a way to alleviate this tension and yield an ultralight axion with a phenomenologically relevant decay constant. Our analysis also identifies lower-action instantons whose contributions must be controlled to preserve the ultralight axion. We then study the resulting class of quintessence potentials and qualitatively compare their late-time cosmological solutions with observationally preferred ranges.

BPS Chaos and Fortuity in Higgs Branches I: The Gepner Point
Micha Berkooz, Trivko Kukolj, Ohad Mamroud et al. 2610.36035v1 [PDF] Score 7
CFTString Theoryhep-th
The BPS states in Calabi-Yau compactifications of type II string theory can sometimes be described using quiver quantum mechanics whose Higgs branch is a complete intersection manifold. Their degeneracy resembles that of BPS black holes. A small fraction of these states corresponds to multi-center supergravity brane configurations and can be continued to the (near-Higgs) Coulomb branch. We identify them as monotone states. The overwhelming majority of states, known as pure-Higgs states, are fortuitous states, which concentrate at a particular $R$-charge. This division has a precise mathematical meaning as two different types of cohomologies of the Higgs branch target space. We argue that a natural probe of BPS chaos in these models is the Berry/Chern curvature. In the case of the Gepner model, we use localization techniques in an auxiliary two-dimensional CFT to compute the curvature, its RMT properties, and deviations from it.

On memory effects in odd spacetime dimensions
Ana-Maria Raclariu, Binsong Xiong 2610.35283v1 [PDF] Score 6
Asymptotic Symmetrieshep-th
We establish the existence of a new electromagnetic memory effect in all odd spacetime dimensions. This effect is a universal property of electromagnetic fields sourced by charged particles of constant momenta, appears at the Coulombic order and is directly related to the Weinberg soft theorem. We provide a unified derivation of this effect in all higher dimensions by evaluating the Maxwell constraint on a null plane at large, finite distance. We observe that in odd spacetime dimensions, the lightray integral computing the memory effect does not commute with the large-$r$ expansion. Our results necessarily assume that the lightray integral is taken prior to the $r \rightarrow \infty$ limit. The effect vanishes in the opposite order of limits, in agreement with previous results. We compute the Coulombic order electromagnetic memory effect for both massless and massive sources in all higher dimensions, explain its relation to the Fourier transform of the leading soft photon theorem and present a protocol for its measurement in a finite time parametrically larger than the distance of a detector from the sources. We conclude with comments on the generalization to gravity.

Resurgence of Black Holes in Thermal OPE
Alba Grassi, Hewei Frederic Jia 2610.37554v1 [PDF] Score 4
AdS/CFTCFThep-th
The momentum-space thermal OPE generically gives an asymptotic high-energy expansion of thermal two-point functions. We study the resurgent structure of this expansion in holographic CFTs at infinite and finite volume, whose dual bulk geometries are planar and spherical AdS black holes, respectively. The perturbative expansion is fixed to all orders by the stress-tensor sector of the thermal OPE and can be computed from a near-boundary analysis of the bulk wave equation. Non-perturbative effects, on the other hand, encode information about the bulk geometry away from the boundary. Using exact WKB analysis, we identify the locations of the associated Borel singularities as WKB actions between the AdS boundary and distinguished points in the complex radial plane, which include the black hole singularity in certain regimes. Furthermore, we identify a complex-frequency chamber in the timelike momentum regime where Borel resummation of the high-energy expansion reconstructs the full thermal two-point function. We further derive Stokes jumps that determine the non-perturbative contributions required upon crossing the boundary of this chamber.

The 't Hooft model on (A)dS$_2$
Panos Betzios 2610.37667v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFThep-th
We solve large-$N$ two-dimensional QCD with fundamental quarks---the 't Hooft model---on rigid AdS$_2$ and dS$_2$. Their isometries preserve the null gauge $A_-=0$ and in affine coordinates the curvature enters the light-front Hamiltonian only through the special-conformal generator of the null line. The $SL(2,\mathbb{R)}$ Casimir then results in an exact meson equation: 't Hooft's equation plus a Legendre operator whose sign is determined by the curvature. On AdS$_2$ it is related to equations proposed in the context of folded/segmented strings and light-front holography. The pion is massless in the chiral limit and obeys a Gell-Mann--Oakes--Renner relation that fixes the chiral condensate, the dimensions cross over from Regge to logarithmic growth once the flux tube exceeds the curvature radius, and the flat-space integrable structure survives. We also solve the problem of a quark interacting with an antiquark placed at the boundary: beyond a critical coupling the string pulls it back to the boundary, resulting in a scattering phase. On dS$_2$ and due to the expansion of space, the mesons are unstable resonances even at $N=\infty$ and eventually dissociate. Their widths are given by Boltzmann factors at the de Sitter temperature.

Limin Zeng 2610.37546v1 [PDF] Score 3

We construct a classical reflected phase space for open Carroll--Weyl null strings with Dirichlet conditions at the target origin or linear mixed target gluings. The reflection-compatible constraints are differentiable, their Hamiltonian flows preserve the boundary domain, and the constraint system is weakly first class on its regular part. The induced boundary algebra extends the boundary Carrollian conformal algebra by an even weight-one Carroll--Weyl current, with normalization fixed by the reflected symplectic form. The regular local embedding count agrees with that of the closed theory. For timelike defects with natural tangential variations and no additional boundary interactions, non-characteristic endpoints have vanishing Carroll flow. For displaced Dirichlet data, we derive the canonical affine lift and its required shift of the edge momentum by integrated bulk momentum.

On the geometric origin of central charges in gravity
Lorenzo Di Giacomo, Daniele Pranzetti 2610.37302v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFTCFThep-thgr-qc
Central extensions of asymptotic symmetry algebras are traditionally determined through an explicit computation of the algebra of conserved charges. We show that, within an ambiguity-free covariant phase space formalism based on the Noetherian split, they instead arise as intrinsic quantities completely determined by the Lagrangian through the geometry of the associated covariant phase space. We prove two general Charge Representation Theorems describing the canonical realization of diffeomorphism symmetries in the presence of field-dependent vector fields and Lagrangian anomalies, corresponding respectively to vanishing and integrable Noetherian flux. In both cases, we derive explicit expressions for the associated central extensions and establish the corresponding cocycle identities. This provides a constructive algorithm for determining central charges directly from the Lagrangian formulation of the theory, without requiring an explicit computation of the charge algebra. As a non-trivial application, we consider asymptotically locally AdS$_3$ spacetimes with Penrose--Brown--Henneaux boundary conditions. We recover the corresponding centrally extended asymptotic symmetry algebra, providing an explicit realization of the Charge Representation Theorems.

Can One Hear the Absence of a Singularity?
Matteo Della Rocca, Paolo Pani, Andrea P. Sanna 2610.37028v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravitygr-qchep-th
One of the central expectations of quantum gravity is the resolution of the curvature singularities predicted by General Relativity, potentially giving rise to effective black-hole geometries that are regular inside the horizon. We show that, for a very broad class of regular black holes including several known solutions, the quasinormal-mode spectrum in the large-overtone limit differs discontinuously from that of a Schwarzschild black hole, even when the regularization parameter is arbitrarily small. The real part of the quasinormal frequencies vanishes asymptotically, rather than approaching a nonzero constant, as generically occurs in General Relativity and other theories predicting black holes with curvature singularities. The asymptotic black-hole spectrum thus retains a distinctive imprint of singularity resolution. We establish these results analytically through a systematic formulation of the monodromy method and confirm them numerically. We interpret this phenomenon as a manifestation of black-hole spectral instability, whereby arbitrarily small deviations from vacuum General Relativity produce nonperturbative changes in the large-overtone spectrum.

We construct reflection-asymmetric thin-shell wormholes by joining a Schwarzschild exterior to its minimal geometric deformation, which shares the same mass and redshift function, so that the asymmetry is carried entirely by the gravitational-decoupling sector. The deformed side is a black hole sourced by a scalar field coupled to nonlinear electrodynamics, in its branch with a real scalar. We show that the total radial null energy of shell and bulk obeys an exact identity and becomes positive for a sufficiently strong deformation. The momentum flux across the shell equals the radial null-energy density of the bulk, and retaining it yields the linear stability condition in closed form. We prove that stability with a conventional shell response requires the bulk to violate the radial null energy condition at the throat; the deformation instead enlarges the stable domain and moves the transition radius inside the photon sphere. For a stationary extension built on Kerr, the junction is solved exactly in the spin, which fixes how the angular momentum is shared between shell and bulk and where the shell behaves as a rotating fluid. A free-precession model provides an estimate of the gravitational-wave signal of the throat.

Bargmann-invariant witness of the graviton commutator
Hiroki Matsui 2610.35253v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravitygr-qchep-thquant-ph
We identify the phase of a Bargmann invariant of two matter-wave interferometers as a quantum gravity signature that cannot be reproduced by any branchwise-definite classical gravitational field. For matter coupled linearly to linearized gravity, the interferometric coherence factorizes exactly into a conservative Newtonian phase, the characteristic functional of the graviton state, and a commutator phase. For Wigner-positive graviton states, the first two are classically reproducible; therefore, decoherence alone cannot certify that gravity is quantized. The Bargmann phase isolates the commutator, which is independent of the graviton state and of all Newtonian interactions and is bounded by which-path decoherence. For gravity, this radiation-reaction effect is sixty orders of magnitude below experimental reach, but trapped-ion analogs can test it at order unity.

Primordial black holes from a type-IIB/F theory inflaton potential

We study the possibility of the formation of primordial black holes (PBHs) using an inflaton potential constructed by stabilizing all Kähler moduli employing both the perturbative and non-perturbative corrections in the large volume scenario in type-IIB/F string theory. We further introduce a small perturbation in one of the Kähler moduli that modifies the existing potential in the field space. The effective correction in the moduli space is made by introducing a localised Gaussian bump, which induces an ultra-slow-roll feature in the inflaton potential during the early cosmological inflation. The evolution of the amplitude of the curvature power spectrum is solved numerically by using the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation, with Bunch-Davies initial conditions. The inclusion of the Gaussian bump produces a sustainable enhancement in the amplitude of curvature power spectra around $k\sim 1.12 \times 10^{9} Mpc^{-1}$ for the formation of PBHs. The abundance of PBHs ($f_{PBH}$) is calculated using the Press-Schechter formalism for different values of critical density contrast and presented in the background of contemporary $f_{PBH}$ bounds and checked for viability with existing microlensing bounds, corresponding to a mass value $10^{-6}M_\odot$ for $δ$ threshold value $δ_c=0.439$. The enhancement of curvature power spectra lies in the sensitivity region of the SKA observations, suggesting an indication that scalar-induced gravity waves may be probed in future observations.

Characteristic gluing to extremal Kerr horizons and the third law of black hole thermodynamics in vacuum
Stefan Czimek 2610.35836v1 [PDF] Score 3
Geometric Gravitymath.APgr-qcmath-ph
We prove a non-perturbative, non-axisymmetric characteristic gluing theorem for the Einstein vacuum equations: every Boyer-Lindquist sphere in the domain of outer communication of a Kerr black hole, in particular every symmetry sphere of a Schwarzschild exterior, can be glued along a single outgoing null hypersurface to a horizon sphere of an extremal Kerr black hole of sufficiently large mass. The gluing is exact at both ends and matches the full sphere data, including the transversal curvature. This proves a conjecture of Kehle and Unger in the extremal case. We first raise the angular momentum to the extremal value by rotating shear pulses of non-axisymmetric gravitational radiation, and then damp the incoming data exponentially along a long non-expanding neck. At the degenerate target horizon we invert the linearised problem at all angular frequencies, using a factorisation of a fourth-order angular operator into an angular Teukolsky-Starobinsky product. As an application, we construct one-ended asymptotically flat vacuum initial data whose maximal development contains an exactly Schwarzschild apparent horizon and, at a later advanced time, an exactly extremal Kerr event horizon. This is the first rigorous counterexample to the third law of black hole thermodynamics in vacuum, and proves a second conjecture of Kehle and Unger.
