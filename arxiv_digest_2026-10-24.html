Quasinormal modes, complex null geodesics and singularity resolution in regular AdS black holes
Heng-Yu Chen, Yasuaki Hikida, Yasutaka Koga 2610.30915v1 [PDF] Score 11
AdS/CFTCFTQuantum Gravityhep-thgr-qc
The black hole singularity is hidden behind its horizon, and it is difficult to determine whether it is resolved by quantum gravity or stringy effects. For an AdS-Schwarzschild black hole, it is known that the two-point function of the dual conformal field theory has a singularity associated with a null geodesic that bounces off the black hole singularity. In this paper, we investigate how the resolution of the black hole singularity is encoded in the dual conformal field theory by considering regular AdS black holes as regularized geometries. Using WKB analysis, we compute the quasinormal modes of regular AdS black holes and read off the dual two-point functions from the modes. We find that the singularity corresponding to the bouncing null geodesic for singular black holes is replaced by a smooth bump associated with a complex, rather than real, null geodesic. The location of the complex geodesic singularity is supported by analyzing the structure of the operator product expansion for the two-point function. Our results provide a holographic signature of black hole singularity resolution and clarify how information about the regular interior is encoded in boundary correlation functions.

Quantum gravity on the bulk cone
Ahmed Almheiri, Matthew Dodelson, Zohar Komargodski 2610.31610v1 [PDF] Score 9
AdS/CFTCFTQuantum Gravityhep-th
Bulk-cone singularities in holographic correlators encode propagation along bulk null geodesics. The prototypical example is an AdS black hole, where incoming null geodesics can wind around the photon sphere and return to the boundary. These singularities appear in real time in the thermal two-point function at finite volume. We show that conformal field theories dual to gravitational theories at finite $G_N$ cannot have such singularities. We rule out these singularities both for individual microstates and for the thermal state. The main thrust of the argument is to invoke an analytic continuation in the temperature. Qualitatively, the singularity resolution is a consequence of the fuzziness of the bulk solution in quantum gravity.

This thesis investigates the perturbative dynamics and phenomenology of compact objects in two approaches to quantum gravity: quadratic gravity and asymptotic safety. Linear perturbations provide a tool to probe the physical properties of spacetimes and their potential observational signatures, including quasinormal modes, black hole shadows, and Hawking radiation. Hidden zeros of tree-level string amplitudes of colored scalars lie on loci specified by integer-valued dimensionless momentum contractions arranged in rectangles on the kinematic mesh. We study the same kinematic loci for open-string amplitudes with gluons, fermions and higher-spin states at arbitrary finite mass levels. Disk monodromy relations yield $\textit{zero-locus factorization}$: at regular points where the monodromy system is invertible, the amplitude is a weighted sum of physical three-point amplitudes sewn to complete on-shell $(n-1)$-point amplitudes. Quantum Gravitygr-qc
We compare the quasinormal modes (QNMs), greybody factors, and Hawking emission of massless scalar, electromagnetic, and Dirac test fields propagating on a non-minimal Einstein--Yang--Mills black hole. The QNMs are calculated using WKB and pseudospectral methods, while greybody factors are estimated with sixth-order WKB method. All computed modes are damped within the parameter range considered. Higher overtones generally exhibit stronger dependence on the non-minimal coupling $ξ$ than the fundamental mode, including non-monotonic behavior in the scalar and Dirac sectors. For large multipole numbers, all three fields follow a common leading eikonal description governed by the unstable circular null orbit. Increasing $ξ$ raises the orbital angular velocity and reduces the Lyapunov exponent, leading to higher oscillation frequencies and weaker damping rate. The greybody factors depend strongly on the multipole number, with weaker field dependent corrections from $ξ$. The partial-wave emission powers generally decrease with increasing $ξ$, while the lowest scalar mode shows mild non-monotonic behavior. This comparison reveals enhanced overtone sensitivity alongside comparatively small changes in transmission, whereas the Hawking emission reflects changes in both the greybody factors and the black hole temperature.

Wormhole geometries and energy conditions in linear and non-linear $f(Q,L_m)$ gravity
Sara Rastgoo, Foad Parsaei 2610.30224v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometrygr-qc
This study explores static and spherically symmetric wormhole configurations in an extended formulation of symmetric teleparallel gravity governed by $f(Q,L_m)=α(-Q)+βL_m $ and $f(Q,L_m)= -Q+L_m^α $ (where $Q$ is the non-metricity scalar, $L_m$ is the matter Lagrangian, and $α, β$ are constants). By varying the action with respect to the metric, we obtain the corresponding field equations. We conclude that the linear $f(Q,L_m)=α(-Q)+βL_m$ framework successfully supports physically realistic wormholes without requiring extreme energy condition violations. It has been demonstrated that spacetime solutions violating both the radial and lateral null energy conditions in general relativity can nevertheless satisfy the NEC in $f(Q, L_m)$ gravity. Within non-linear $f(Q,L_m)$ gravity, non-exotic asymptotically flat wormhole solutions are derived using linear and asymptotically linear equations of state. Our results open new avenues to link modified gravity models with the observable astrophysical traits of exotic spacetimes.

Rotation slows the instability of Ellis-Bronnikov wormholes
Nikita M. Shirokov, Ilya Nachevsky 2610.29941v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometrygr-qcastro-ph.HE
We present nonlinear 3+1 evolutions of rotating Ellis-Bronnikov wormholes. We construct stationary rotating drainhole solutions with a spectral solver up to an equatorial rotation velocity of 99% of the speed of light, validate the solver against published symmetric solutions, and correct an inconsistency in a published field equation. Perturbed only by truncation error, the evolutions show that rotation slows the radial instability of the static wormhole. Near 30% of the extremal angular momentum the unperturbed throat expands instead of collapsing, rather than the instability disappearing as second-order perturbation theory suggests. From 58% upward we find no evidence of instability over the evolved times; at the fastest spin this holds at three resolutions. Collapsing rotating throats emit an axisymmetric quadrupolar gravitational-wave burst whose energy grows with spin. Second-order perturbative initial data trigger the instability through their own truncation error, so they are unsuitable for stability studies.

A spinor-adapted geometric approach to nonlinear Dirac systems on the Schwarzschild black hole background
Seokchang Hong 2610.29406v1 [PDF] Score 3
Geometric Gravitymath.APmath-ph
Building on our previous work, we extend the spinor-adapted geometric method for nonlinear Dirac systems from small perturbations of Minkowski spacetime to the Schwarzschild background. As a linear foundation, we establish integrated local energy decay estimates and an \(r^p\)-weighted energy hierarchy for spinor fields, adapting the physical-space approach of Dafermos and Rodnianski to the Dirac setting. The main focus of the paper is nonlinear. We consider a semilinear tensorial wave--Dirac model motivated by the wave-gauge reduction of the Einstein--Dirac system and prove global small-data stability. A principal difficulty is the apparent loss of derivatives arising from the coupling between the first-order Dirac equation and the second-order tensorial wave equation. We overcome this difficulty through a spinor-adapted energy hierarchy that exploits the complementary roles of the Dirac-current energy and the spinorial wave energy. In particular, the nonlinear estimates are closed without requiring the top-order Dirac-current estimate. The nonlinear analysis demonstrates that the spinorial structures remain essential on the black-hole background. The combination of Dirac-current energy, spinorial wave energy, Kosmann Lie derivatives, and the Clifford-algebraic null structure enables us to close the nonlinear estimates without imposing an independent null condition on the tensorial wave equation. Thus, the nonlinear closure relies fundamentally on structures intrinsic to the Dirac equation. A second main result is the global small-data stability of the Maxwell--Dirac system in the Lorenz gauge on the Schwarzschild background, under a vanishing-charge assumption. This extends the nonlinear stability theory to a genuine gauge-coupled Dirac system and demonstrates the applicability of the spinor-adapted physical-space approach beyond the tensorial wave--Dirac model.
