#1
A Common Type II Origin of Galilean and Carroll Superstrings
Mehmet Ozkan, Utku Zorba 2610.41582v1 [PDF] Score 9
AdS/CFTString Theoryhep-thgr-qc
We show that the relativistic Type-II superalgebras, once the fundamental-string winding charge is retained alongside the momentum, admit a single non-Lorentzian contraction whose two translation ideals carry Galilean and magnetic-Carroll supersymmetry, respectively. The complementary vector charge required for this structure is thus intrinsic to string theory. The IIA realization is particle-like and the IIB realization string-like, a distinction fixed by chirality, and spacelike T-duality relates the two while preserving each non-Lorentzian structure. The same contraction is finite on the doubled Green--Schwarz action, including all fermion terms and local kappa symmetry, where the two supersymmetries appear as the two polarizations of a single worldsheet theory. These results unify two distinct non-Lorentzian corners of string theory within a common Type-II origin and open a route toward a broader web of non-Lorentzian dualities and possible holographic generalizations.
#2
Area laws for free fermions in ergodic field: spectral criteria and localisation-based methods
Leonid Pastur, Mira Shamis 2610.41845v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometrymath-phmath.SP
We review rigorous results concerning the Area Law for the entanglement entropy of free fermions whose one-body Hamiltonian is an ergodic Schrödinger operator with a strongly localised pure point spectrum. Particular attention is paid to operators with deterministic potentials (quasi-periodic ones and those generated by subshifts of finite type). Our approach relies on reducing the problem to obtaining exponential estimates for the eigenfunction correlator. This provides an effective route from localisation to the Area Law and allows for the identification of additional spectral estimates that need to be proven for various classes of ergodic operators. We realise this approach by using various localisation techniques, including dynamical localisation and the uniformly localised eigenfunction property for bounded and unbounded ergodic operators (e.g. the Maryland model), as well as a new method for obtaining the corresponding exponential bounds for Schrödinger operators generated by subshifts of finite type.
