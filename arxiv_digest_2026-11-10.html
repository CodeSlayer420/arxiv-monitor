Mozib Bin Awal, Prabwal Phukon 2611.11889v1 [PDF] Score 11
AdS/CFTCFThep-th
We investigate critical relaxation dynamics and their connection to quantum information scrambling in the conformal field theory dual to a Reissner-Nordström anti-de Sitter (RN-AdS) black hole. Employing the holographic dictionary, we formulate the thermodynamics in terms of boundary CFT variables and study the stochastic evolution of the reduced horizon radius using Langevin and Fokker-Planck formalisms. We demonstrate pronounced critical slowing down in the dual CFT, characterized by an enhancement of the autocorrelation time and stochastic fluctuations, accompanied by a suppression of the lowest nonzero Fokker-Planck eigenvalue. The autocorrelation time exhibits a universal power-law divergence, $τ\sim|ε|^{-Δ}$, with $Δ\simeq 2/3$, consistent with the analytical prediction and previous bulk gravitational results. Going beyond the conventional single-parameter analysis, we incorporate variations of the CFT central charge and identify a combined scaling variable involving temperature and central charge, demonstrating that the leading relaxation dynamics retain the same critical exponent along different trajectories in the two-dimensional parameter space. Finally, we explore the interplay between thermodynamic relaxation and quantum information scrambling by comparing the relaxation timescale with the fast-scrambling timescale. The comparison reveals a crossover between thermodynamic relaxation and quantum scrambling for which we derive the corresponding crossover scale analytically and find that, in the large-central-charge limit, it behaves as $\hatε\sim(C\ln C)^{-3/2}$. We also verify this numerically using the full nonlinear free-energy landscape. Our results connect critical dynamical scaling, finite-central-charge effects, and quantum scrambling within the holographic CFT framework.

What an Observer Reads off a Holographic Screen
Kenan Oggad, James F. Glazebrook, Chris Fields 2611.11356v1 [PDF] Score 6
AdS/CFTEntanglement & Geometryquant-phgr-qc
What does an observer read off a holographic screen that is an array of qubits rather than a surface? In the generalized holographic principle the interaction between two separable systems acts on such a screen as a weighted sum, up to an overall prefactor, of commuting observables with values plus and minus one. When the weights are nonnegative and the prefactor is one and the joint eigenspaces are lines, everything a probe reads off the spectrum depends only on the list of weights, which we call the profile. On the screen with equal weights summing to one the running dimension read by a diffusion probe falls short of two at unit time by at most twice the reciprocal of the number of qubits; a geometric profile gives instead, through its level count, a plateau of any positive height. An observer correlates sets of qubits; its readout carries a profile at a given weighting when the levels it reads, counted with multiplicity, are those of a screen with one weight per set. We prove that a readout carries a profile at every weighting exactly when the distinct nonzero traces of its qubits are linearly independent over the field with two elements, the trace of a qubit being the list of sets that contain it; such a readout is a partition of the qubits it covers up to an invertible recoding, and a dependent one carries a profile only on finitely many proper linear subspaces of weightings. Refining a grouping without end builds a tower whose boundary is a profinite set and hence a condensed set, on which a strictly positive compatible profile separates the locally constant functions by a distance. Applications certify probabilistic and strong contextuality and give the leading shortfall of the distance between the poles of the fuzzy sphere; in a model of monogamous entanglement the pairs that weight-one threads representing operational ER=EPR can occupy at once are exactly the partial matchings.

Liberation in the Heavy Limit
Sridip Pal, Gordon Rogelberg 2611.11594v1 [PDF] Score 5
AdS/CFTCFThep-thcond-mat.stat-mechmath-ph
We study CFT four-point functions of identical scalar primaries as their dimension $Δ$ grows for fixed $0<z, \bar z<1$, and call them liberated if they grow at the same exponential rate as those of a generalized free field (GFF), as is the case for heavy free particles propagating along geodesics in AdS. We show that the correlator is liberated at the crossing symmetric point if and only if the light spectrum is sparse, and that liberation then holds wherever $z$ and $\bar z$ lie on the same side of $1/2$. If the low twist states are also sparse, liberation holds everywhere except in an explicit band around $z+\bar z=1$. In particular, if the primary twist gap lies above $\sqrt{2}Δ$, then the correlator is liberated everywhere. For twist cutoffs below $Δ$, composites of two spinning generalized free fields fail to be liberated in a region that fills the band near the double lightcone limit. In two dimensions, Virasoro primaries cannot be liberated unless the central charge grows faster than $Δ$. The same framework sharpens the universality of large-$c$ torus partition functions of two-dimensional CFTs.

We study the quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a minimally coupled massless scalar field on the exact spinning Morris-Thorne wormhole. The problem is posed on the complete two-ended geometry, whose asymptotic rest frames rotate relative to each other. After extracting the axis behaviour and the outgoing phases at both infinities, we compactify the radial coordinate and construct a quadratic eigenvalue problem by two-dimensional Chebyshev collocation. The analytic background yields exact constraints on the spectrum. At fixed azimuthal number $m$, the frequencies $\widehatω=r_0ω$ are mirror symmetric about $\operatorname{Re}\widehatω=3πjm/2$, where $r_0$ is the throat radius and $j=J/r_0^2$ is the dimensionless angular momentum. Each multipole family depends on $m$ and $j$ only through $mj$ and three angular Chebyshev polynomials represent exactly the multipoles $L=|m|,|m|+1,|m|+2$. A conserved flux confines nonthreshold real quasinormal frequencies to the superradiant window $0<ω/(mΩ_-)<1$, where $Ω_-=3πJ/r_0^3$. For the sampled sectors $m=1,2,3$ and spins up to $j=19$, every converged frequency is damped and lies outside this window. Independent integration verifies the low-lying modes, while Kerr spectra provide a separate benchmark for the two-dimensional solver. A limiting radial equation explains why the computed mode frequencies approach the upper edge of the superradiant window with frequency offsets proportional to $(mj)^{-1/2}$ at large $mj$. A complete stability assessment requires further analysis of the unresolved scalar spectrum and of perturbations of the coupled metric-matter system.

On Thermodynamic Role of the Gauss-Bonnet Topological Term
Marek Liška, David Kubiznak 2611.12698v1 [PDF] Score 3
Geometric Gravityhep-thgr-qc
We revisit the thermodynamic implications of the Gauss-Bonnet topological term in four spacetime dimensions. It has been previously claimed in the literature that this term, although it does not modify the equations of motion, shifts the black hole entropy by a contribution proportional to the Euler characteristic of the horizon. We argue that this conclusion is sensitive to the choice of the boundary action on the horizon. We then identify a choice for which the Wald entropy expression naturally reproduces the Jacobson-Myers entropy, even for non-stationary black holes. We also show that there are no further contributions to conserved quantities from smooth outer boundaries, regardless of the choice of boundary conditions there and of the asymptotic behaviour of the spacetime. Moreover, we demonstrate that the topological Gauss-Bonnet term changes the critical temperature of the Hawking-Page phase transition, opening, in principle, a way for detecting its presence in the gravitational action. In fact, for a positive Gauss-Bonnet coupling constant, we observe a zeroth-order Hawking phase transition at high temperatures and pressures. While our discussion focuses on the case of the Gauss-Bonnet term in 4D, the results are more general and qualitatively apply to other Lovelock Lagrangians in their respective critical dimensions.
