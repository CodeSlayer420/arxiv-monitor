Yang Xiao, Yuxiao Xie 2610.22170v1 [PDF] Score 13
CFTmath.PRmath-ph
Compactified Imaginary Liouville Theory (CILT for short) is a probabilistically constructed Conformal Field Theory (CFT), expected to have connections to minimal models, Coulomb gas integrals, and loop models. In this paper, we study the quantization of Segal's semigroup given by CILT. Each element $\mathbf T_f$ in the quantized semigroup corresponds to a conformal annulus: the unit disk $\mathbb D$ with $f(\mathbb D^\circ)$ removed, where $f:\mathbb D\to f(\mathbb D)\subset \mathbb D^\circ$ is a contracting biholomorphism. This semigroup encodes the local conformal structure of the CFT, in particular the Virasoro symmetry with central charge $<1$ for CILT. Using Segal's gluing axiom as in the real case, we differentiate the amplitude and use conformal covariance to derive the Ward identities in terms of all Virasoro operators $\mathbf L_n,\widetilde{\mathbf L}_n$ ($n\in\mathbb Z$). We also outline the parallel theory for Boundary CILT (BCILT), concerning Segal's semigroup of semiannuli. These results provide essential tools for the conformal bootstrap program of (B)CILT, boundary BPZ equations, boundary structure constants, and the global construction of conformal blocks.

AdS/CFTCFTString Theoryhep-th
The RNS formalism describing string theory on $\mathrm{AdS}_3 \times \mathbf{S}^3 \times \mathrm{K3}$ supported by pure NS-NS flux is in principle straightforward to write down. One can directly check that the worldsheet theory reproduces the correct chiral spectrum expected from supergravity by analyzing the BRST cohomology. However, when one attempts to construct the worldsheet partition function using the standard rules of the RNS formalism by appropriately summing over spin structures to ensure worldsheet modular invariance, one finds a result in tension with spacetime supersymmetry. We also point out the worldsheet construction of a non-supersymmetric theory, whose spectrum is free of tachyons.

Marten Reehorst 2610.20701v1 [PDF] Score 9
CFThep-thcond-mat.stat-mechmath-ph
Numerical conformal bootstrap bounds are commonly called rigorous, yet as published they are not theorems: they hold at sampled points rather than on regions of parameter space, for truncated rational approximations of the conformal blocks, for a finite list of spins, and up to floating-point rounding. We close all four gaps. We show how to search efficiently for functionals positive on regions, including external-dimension space; how positivity on the large-spin crossing vectors can be imposed as such a region constraint; how new analytic bounds on the conformal-block recursion relations give functionals positive on the true, untruncated crossing vectors; and how to certify the positivity of these functionals in exact arithmetic. The analytic inputs for the mixed $σ$-$ε$ system, which would remove the spin-2 gap assumption, are also proved. The same methods prevent common practical failure modes and can make bootstrap searches faster: region bounds turn the mapping of an island into a handful of one-dimensional bisections. The essential code to reproduce the search and the certification, and all machine-checkable certificates, are released.

We study the bound states perturbations in the interior of the Schwarzschild black hole and examine whether or not their microstates spectrum can furnish a statistical description of the black hole entropy. These states contain a discrete set of modes characterized by the labels $(s, p,\ell,m)$, where $s$ represents the spin of the species, $p$ denotes the radial excitation number while $(\ell,m)$ are the usual angular momentum labels. Requiring that the shortest wavelength resolved by the highly excited bound states remains above the quantum gravity scale introduces an angular cutoff $\ell_{\rm max}\sim r_H/L_{\rm Pl}$. The resulting angular degeneracy therefore scales with the horizon area. We count the corresponding configurations in the microcanonical ensemble and find an entropy of the form $S_{\rm bs}=αA/\hbar G$. Demanding that the bound state sector to be in thermal equilibrium with the black hole at the Hawking temperature fixes the fraction of the bound state energy in total black hole energy. We also examine the conditions under which the bound states could account for the full black hole entropy. Although such saturation is possible at the level of state counting, it requires an order unity fraction in the black hole energy and therefore the backreactions on the background geometry cannot be ignored.

Quantum Gravitygr-qc
Gravitational lensing is usually treated as an initial value problem: for a given impact parameter of an unbound orbit, one computes the resulting deflection angle. In this paper we advocate the inverse point of view. For a prescribed deflection angle, the orbit is selected by two boundary conditions at spatial infinity, so that the lensing problem becomes a well-defined two-point boundary value problem, with the impact parameter playing the role of a nonlinear eigenvalue. We show that pseudo-spectral methods based on Chebyshev collocation solve this boundary value problem accurately at modest computational cost, and that the parallel transport equations governing the spin precession of the particle reduce to a single linear system on the same grid. As a concrete application, we compute the spin precession of massive particles in backward scattering off two black hole spacetimes from loop quantum gravity: the covariant effective metric and the self-dual loop quantum black hole. All equations are written for a general static, spherically symmetric line element with three free functions, so that both spacetimes are handled on the same footing. We find that the quantum corrections are most pronounced for slowly moving particles, while in the ultra-relativistic limit the universal spin flip is always recovered. The flip itself is universal, but the rate at which it is approached is not: the derivative of $\cosξ$ with respect to the particle speed at $v=1$ is a strong-field quantity in every backward channel and depends clearly on the quantum parameters of both spacetimes. The framework is not restricted to the models studied here and applies to any static, spherically symmetric spacetime.

On Algebraic Background-Independence
Luka Vinck, Sebastian De Haro 2610.22042v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravityphysics.hist-phgr-qchep-th
It has recently been claimed that algebraic methods can be used to give a background-independent description of semiclassical gravity. This paper aims to provide an entry point into recent algebraic approaches to quantum gravity and to analyse and critically assess the conception of background-independence here involved. We discuss an algebraic framework for formulating an algebra of observables along the worldline of an observer in a cosmological setting. By 'gravitationally dressing' the observables to the observer's worldline, the reference to the spacetime background can be set aside at the level of the abstract *-algebra. Thus this *-algebra is taken to be independent of any particular spacetime realisation, with different states on the algebra corresponding to different backgrounds. We compare this algebraic framework to three influential notions of background-independence in the literature and argue that none of these fully captures the sense in which the *-algebra is taken to be background-independent. Building on this, we extract a novel notion of algebraic background-independence tied to the representation-independence of the *-algebra. This algebraic conception motivates two further concepts of background-independence tailored to semiclassical theories: semiclassical background-independence and perturbative background-independence. We suggest that these provide useful tools for evaluating the scope and limitations of semiclassical approaches to quantum gravity.

The Magic Barrier: A Universal Feature of Quantum Thermalization
Jakob J. Minar, Andreas Schäfer, Leonhard Schmotzer et al. 2610.20768v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometryhep-thcond-mat.stat-mechquant-ph
We show that the emergence of a ``magic barrier'', characterized by a transient maximum of nonstabilizerness during rapid entanglement growth, is a universal feature of thermalization within quantum ergodic theories, which is intimately connected to random matrix spectral properties. Starting from a pure product state, we analytically determine the time evolution of entanglement entropy, antiflatness, capacity of entanglement, and nonlocal trace distance magic under unitary dynamics generated by random matrix Hamiltonians drawn from all three Gaussian ensembles. We find that the transient maxima of antiflatness, capacity of entanglement, and nonlocal magic occur on the same characteristic timescale as the maximum rate of entanglement growth. These results establish that the magic barrier is a universal feature of RMT thermalization, suggesting universality across quantum chaotic systems. We conclude that quantitative simulations of thermalization in ergodic quantum theories inherently require quantum computation.

Infrared-Finite Positivity Bounds in 4D Gravity
Ziyu Dong 2610.19149v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravityhep-thhep-ph
We formulate an S-matrix bootstrap in an exactly flat half-space, where boundary reflection provides geometric infrared control for four-dimensional gravity. Under the channel positivity conditions, we obtain an infrared-finite positivity bound on local four-scalar Wilson coefficients that includes a finite gravitational correction of order $G_N$ and is derived from the full UV absorptive spectrum without specifying a UV theory, imposing a spectral ansatz, requiring weak coupling or expanding the spectrum perturbatively. At fixed dimensionless external state parameters, the coefficient of the leading gravitational correction is independent of the EFT scale and requires neither an arbitrary soft resolution scale nor a model-dependent UV scale. Gaussian wave-packet scattering localizes interactions within the flat region and directly probes bulk Wilson coefficients while exponentially suppressing sensitivity to the boundary. Long-range gravitons remain sensitive to the boundary and give a finite contribution. The smearing functional passes numerical positivity tests with general half-space absorptive kernels in both crossed channels. An explicit string example shows how boundary reflection suppresses the collective contribution of string resonances to the absorptive part of the amplitude, connecting infrared control of low-energy scattering to the UV spectrum of quantum gravity in the same dispersion relation. The framework opens a route to infrared-finite constraints on effective interactions across particle physics and gravity.

Gravity from generalized entropy

Entanglement & GeometryGeometric Gravitygr-qchep-thquant-ph
Gravity from entropy (GfE) identifies its Lagrangian with the geometric quantum relative entropy (GQRE) between the spacetime metric and a metric induced by matter and curvature, treating the metric itself as a quantum operator. We extend this construction to the Tsallis, Rényi, and Sharma-Mittal families, which we call gravity from generalized entropy (GfGE), and determine their common Einstein limit, weak-field spectra, auxiliary-field formulation, and horizon entropy. The entropy choice controls the leading higher-curvature terms and additional modes. We confirm that the linearized GfE spectrum contains a healthy scalar and a Planck-scale tachyonic spin-2 ghost. We identify the conditions that remove this ghost: curvature must enter through its scalar trace, or the entropy generator must be flat to quadratic order at the identity. The latter gives exact general relativity for Tsallis at $q=0$ and ghost-free $f(R,L_m)$ theories for Rényi and Sharma-Mittal. On shell, the auxiliary G-field is proportional to the tensor in Wald's entropy formula. For analytic entropy functions, horizons with a single curvature scale have an area term followed by constant and inverse-area corrections. We also extend the volume-constraint cosmological constant to the full family; its leading entropy dependence is set by the scalar mass. A finite set of entropy-function derivatives controls the leading weak-curvature departures from general relativity.
