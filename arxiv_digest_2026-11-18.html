Cosmic Censorship in the Landscape of SUSY-Broken AdS Vacua
Netta Engelhardt, Richard Nally 2611.21722v1 [PDF] Score 15
AdS/CFTEntanglement & GeometryGeometric GravityString Theoryhep-thgr-qc
We investigate signatures of weak cosmic censorship in non-supersymmetric vacua of both IIA and IIB string theory. In particular, we examine the Penrose Inequality, which can be proven holographically using Ryu-Takayanagi, and can be independently proven in SUSY AdS vacua for spherically symmetric initial data. We prove that any EFT of scalars coupled to gravity in AdS, including in particular those of string compactifications, satisfies both the Penrose Inequality and a positive mass theorem if a function that we call a "weak fake superpotential" exists. We demonstrate that this condition is satisfied globally for the DGKT skew-whiffed vacua in IIA. Since there is no known nonperturbative instability in this DGKT vacuum, this provides evidence that the IIA SUSY-broken AdS vacua may be absolutely stable. We also find the weak fake superpotential exists locally in field space in some SUSY-broken AdS vacua in a KKLT construction in IIB. Thus, within the framework considered here, some false SUSY-broken KKLT AdS vacua are stable against vacuum decay. Conversely, we find that some KKLT vacua do not have a weak fake superpotential, and correspondingly violate the Penrose Inequality and fail to have a positive mass theorem; the DGKT vacua only violate the Penrose Inequality in the presence of designer gravity boundary conditions. In all cases studied, this is due to false vacuum decay. We discuss the implications of these violations for holography and black hole thermodynamics in these SUSY-broken AdS vacua.

The additive ambiguity of gravitational entropy fixed by the topology of the horizon
Stylianos A. Tsilioukas 2611.22731v1 [PDF] Score 8
AdS/CFTGeometric GravityQuantum Gravitygr-qchep-th
In quantum gravity the algebra of observables of a subregion is of type III$_1$ at leading order in the semiclassical expansion, and it becomes of type II once the gravitational constraint is imposed. Hence generalized entropy acquires a trace, however it is defined only up to an additive constant. In this work we argue that in four-dimensional Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet gravity this constant is the Gauss--Bonnet contribution to the Wald entropy, and that it is carried by the normalization of the trace and not by the state. In particular, the conical expansion of the Gauss--Bonnet density is exactly linear in the deficit angle, and on smooth squashed cones it reduces to the intrinsic curvature of the cut, so that the term is a constant on every smooth cross-section and carries no boost energy. Its Noether charge is invariant under all deformations within a topological sector and central in any algebra that preserves the sector, and thus a state-independent constant on such an algebra can only rescale the trace. Additionally, when the boundary of the subregion is completed as the Euler action requires, the Gauss--Bonnet term does not contribute to the presymplectic form, the crossed product is inherited from Einstein gravity, and the constant is the Euler number of the excised Euclidean tip. We state the hypotheses, we weaken factoriality to a central decomposition, and we illustrate the mechanism in Jackiw--Teitelboim gravity and in topological black holes in anti-de Sitter space, where the topological weight displaces the Hawking--Page temperature and a black-hole merger shifts the coarse-grained boundary entropy by a state-independent amount.

Yuri Chervonyi, Fabian Ruehle, Nenad Tomasev 2611.21751v1 [PDF] Score 6
String Theoryhep-thcs.AI
Standard Model fermion masses exhibit large hierarchies, spanning more than five orders of magnitude in the quark sector alone. In this paper, we study whether these hierarchies can be generated perturbatively at tree level in smooth heterotic Calabi-Yau compactifications, where the supergravity approximation remains under computational control. In such backgrounds, physical Yukawa couplings combine holomorphic superpotential integrals with matter field Kahler metrics. The former can be computed algebraically, while the latter require solving a coupled system of partial differential equations for the Ricci-flat metric, Hermitian Yang-Mills connections, and harmonic bundle-valued forms. Within this framework, we identify two new tree-level mechanisms by which complex structure moduli can generate fermion mass hierarchies and show analytically and numerically that both mechanisms are obstructed for previously studied tetra-quadric models. Motivated by these obstructions, we scan the existing databases of tens of thousands of complete intersection Calabi-Yau line bundle models and find a unique smooth quotient admitting a rank-three up-quark Yukawa matrix at tree level. Evaluating the physical couplings on this manifold and evolving them under the renormalization group, we obtain realistic mass hierarchies for the top, charm, up, and bottom quarks in the smooth supergravity regime.

The python's lunch conjecture relates the complexity of bulk reconstruction in holographic tensor networks to an exponential cost associated with post-selection. We establish a tight query lower bound for this decoding problem in a Haar random oracle model, with a python's lunch circuit that applies Haar random unitary $U$ to a logical input and fixed ancillas, followed by post-selection on $m$ output qubits. Metric solution spaces defined by gauge and boundary conditions are associated with a residual symmetry algebra, consisting of the diffeomorphism generators whose action preserves these conditions. Describing a metric solution space in terms of tetrad variables adds a local Lorentz freedom. When part of this freedom is fixed by additional tetrad gauge and boundary conditions, metric residual symmetries must be supplemented by compensating Lorentz transformations. If a residual Lorentz freedom remains, there is an ambiguity in these compensating transformations. The most direct choices generally fail to realize the metric residual symmetry algebra as a subalgebra of the tetrad one, because of a field-dependent cocycle. We show that a reference tetrad, that is a section of the tetrad solution space seen as a principal bundle over the metric one, always provides a lift free of this cocycle, so that the tetrad residual symmetry algebra is the semi-direct sum of the metric one and the ideal of field-dependent residual Lorentz transformations. The construction is independent of the gauge conditions, of the residual group, of the first-order Lagrangian, and reduces to the Kosmann lift on metric isometry generators. We illustrate it on a Weyl BMS-type solution space with time-dependent boundary metric, described in tetrad variables with a residual U(1) Lorentz freedom, for which the cocycle of a naive lift is computed and then explicitly removed.
