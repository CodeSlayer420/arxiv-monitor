Ankit Anand, Kimet Jusufi, Amir A. Khodahami et al. 2611.24779v1 [PDF] Score 20

We explore the zero-point length $l_0$ of T-duality-inspired effective field theory in holography, distinguishing two readings. As a boundary resolution scale, $l_0$ filters the source of a bulk scalar in anti-de Sitter space and yields an exactly solvable boundary response that is ultraviolet finite without a hard cutoff and inherits the power-law tails of the zero-point-length kernel. We show that this filter is the source-normalized bulk-to-boundary transfer function of a $ν=1$ scalar evaluated at radial depth $z=l_0$. Anchoring Ryu-Takayanagi surfaces on the same radial surface gives finite entanglement entropies that retain the perimeter law and the universal sphere free energy. For two intervals in AdS$_3$, the prescription yields the mutual-information connectivity transition in closed form for arbitrary interval lengths; equal intervals maximize the connectivity window at fixed total length, and in the subresolution regime the critical separation exhibits a universal scaling law with a rescaled mutual information collapsing onto a piecewise-linear profile. As an AdS-invariant bulk proper length, $l_0$ renders the one-loop tadpole finite while preserving the conformal form of boundary two-point functions. Its exact Källén-Lehmann decomposition, however, has negative continuum weight beginning at threshold, obstructing a unitary interpretation of this propagator. The ratio $L/l_0$ identifies a crossover scale of the form factor rather than a derived spectrum of a unitary completion.

ER=EPR and IMP wormholes in quantum gravity
Eyoab Bahiru 2611.23396v1 [PDF] Score 20
AdS/CFTCFTEntanglement & GeometryQuantum Gravityhep-th
We analyze and refine the ER=EPR conjecture by studying IMP (Isenberg, Mazzeo and Pollack \cite{Isenberg:2001qe}) wormholes in QFT and quantum gravity, in the AdS/CFT context. IMP wormholes are solutions to the Einstein equations constructed from another general solution of Einstein's equations by gluing a handle on a Cauchy slice with a constant mean curvature, so that the initial data on the slice does not change in the regions away from the handle. This is made possible by using what is called the conformal method. This same method also enables one to recover a well defined perturbation theory in quantum gravity, and particularly for asymptotically AdS spacetimes \cite{Witten:2022xxp}. Thus we construct the perturbation theory on these backgrounds in addition to exploring ER=EPR when $G_{N}=0$, in the limit it is perturbatively small and beyond perturbation theory.

Netta Engelhardt, Richard Nally 2611.21722v1 [PDF] Score 15
AdS/CFTEntanglement & GeometryGeometric GravityString Theoryhep-thgr-qc
We investigate signatures of weak cosmic censorship in non-supersymmetric vacua of both IIA and IIB string theory. In particular, we examine the Penrose Inequality, which can be proven holographically using Ryu-Takayanagi, and can be independently proven in SUSY AdS vacua for spherically symmetric initial data. We prove that any EFT of scalars coupled to gravity in AdS, including in particular those of string compactifications, satisfies both the Penrose Inequality and a positive mass theorem if a function that we call a "weak fake superpotential" exists. We demonstrate that this condition is satisfied globally for the DGKT skew-whiffed vacua in IIA. Conversely, we find that some KKLT vacua do not have a weak fake superpotential, and correspondingly violate the Penrose Inequality and fail to have a positive mass theorem; the DGKT vacua only violate the Penrose Inequality in the presence of designer gravity boundary conditions. In all cases studied, this is due to false vacuum decay. We discuss the implications of these violations for holography and black hole thermodynamics in these SUSY-broken AdS vacua.

Deep learning spacetime from entanglement dynamics
Hyun-Sik Jeong, Keun-Young Kim, Gaya Yun et al. 2611.24666v1 [PDF] Score 8
AdS/CFTEntanglement & Geometryhep-th
In the eternal AdS black hole, the entanglement of the thermofield double state is geometrically realised by the Einstein--Rosen bridge, in line with the ER=EPR conjecture. Hartman--Maldacena surfaces threading this bridge pass behind the event horizon, so the time-dependent entanglement entropy $S(t)$ of a pair of half-lines, one on each boundary, probes the black hole interior. We turn this relation around and ask how much of the bulk spacetime can be reconstructed from $S(t)$, using a neural ordinary differential equation that learns the metric directly from the entanglement data. We show that $S(t)$ depends on the geometry only through a single function, the local surface gravity expressed in terms of the timelike Killing norm. This is enough to fix a metric with one unknown function: for charged BTZ black holes, the network reconstructs it throughout the region probed by the surfaces, including part of the interior, and the learned metric also yields the Hawking temperature and the location of the critical surface that bounds the accessible interior. Finally, although the null energy condition is not imposed, the network reproduces data obeying the causal bound $v_E\le 1$ on the entanglement velocity, but not data that violate it. Our results thus offer a data-driven perspective on how spacetime, including the black hole interior, emerges from quantum entanglement, and delineate which features of the geometry entanglement alone can determine.

The additive ambiguity of gravitational entropy fixed by the topology of the horizon
Stylianos A. Tsilioukas 2611.22731v1 [PDF] Score 8
AdS/CFTGeometric GravityQuantum Gravitygr-qchep-th
In quantum gravity the algebra of observables of a subregion is of type III$_1$ at leading order in the semiclassical expansion, and it becomes of type II once the gravitational constraint is imposed. Hence generalized entropy acquires a trace, however it is defined only up to an additive constant. In this work we argue that in four-dimensional Einstein--Gauss--Bonnet gravity this constant is the Gauss--Bonnet contribution to the Wald entropy, and that it is carried by the normalization of the trace and not by the state. In particular, the conical expansion of the Gauss--Bonnet density is exactly linear in the deficit angle, and on smooth squashed cones it reduces to the intrinsic curvature of the cut, so that the term is a constant on every smooth cross-section and carries no boost energy. Its Noether charge is invariant under all deformations within a topological sector and central in any algebra that preserves the sector, and thus a state-independent constant on such an algebra can only rescale the trace. Additionally, when the boundary of the subregion is completed as the Euler action requires, the Gauss--Bonnet term does not contribute to the presymplectic form, the crossed product is inherited from Einstein gravity, and the constant is the Euler number of the excised Euclidean tip. We state the hypotheses, we weaken factoriality to a central decomposition, and we illustrate the mechanism in Jackiw--Teitelboim gravity and in topological black holes in anti-de Sitter space, where the topological weight displaces the Hawking--Page temperature and a black-hole merger shifts the coarse-grained boundary entropy by a state-independent amount.

In this paper, we study whether these hierarchies can be generated perturbatively at tree level in smooth heterotic Calabi-Yau compactifications, where the supergravity approximation remains under computational control. In such backgrounds, physical Yukawa couplings combine holomorphic superpotential integrals with matter field Kahler metrics. Describing a metric solution space in terms of tetrad variables adds a local Lorentz freedom. When part of this freedom is fixed by additional tetrad gauge and boundary conditions, metric residual symmetries must be supplemented by compensating Lorentz transformations. If a residual Lorentz freedom remains, there is an ambiguity in these compensating transformations. The most direct choices generally fail to realize the metric residual symmetry algebra as a subalgebra of the tetrad one, because of a field-dependent cocycle.
