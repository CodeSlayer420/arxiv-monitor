#1
Reflected entropy and islands in JT gravity from partial dimensional reduction
Debarshi Basu, Himanshu Chourasiya 2610.13900v1 [PDF] Score 15
AdS/CFTEntanglement & GeometryQuantum Gravityhep-th
We study how mixed-state correlations reorganize across island transitions in Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity coupled to a non-gravitating bath. Using the partial-dimensional-reduction realization of double holography, we compute reflected entropy for adjacent and disjoint radiation regions in the vacuum and in the thermofield-double state of an eternal black hole. Reflected entropy probes the internal partition of the entanglement wedge and therefore resolves structure that is invisible to the ordinary Page curve: its dominant saddle can change even when the entropy saddle of the union remains fixed, and it vanishes at leading order when the entanglement wedge disconnects. We show that the lower-dimensional reflected-island description agrees, to leading order in the dynamical brane fluctuation, with the higher-dimensional defect-extremal-surface picture. The finite-angle comparison further suggests a projection-covariant identification of island and brane endpoints at linear order. The resulting Page-like curves and Markov gaps provide a geometric probe of the reorganization of the purification and of non-Markovian, multipartite correlation structure during island transitions.

Codimension-Zero Holographic Complexity and Spectral Three-Point Correlators
Masamichi Miyaji, Shan-Ming Ruan, Shono Shibuya et al. 2610.13289v1 [PDF] Score 11
AdS/CFTEntanglement & GeometryQuantum Gravityhep-thgr-qc
Holographic complexity is expected to capture the linear growth of black hole interiors, with quantum corrections ultimately leading to a late-time saturation. We develop a spectral formulation of holographic complexity measures associated with codimension-zero bulk regions in the framework of the complexity=anything proposal. The corresponding generating function of complexity is governed by three-point correlation functions of the density of states, establishing a direct connection between bulk geometric observables and nonperturbative Euclidean spacetime wormholes. We find that the codimension-zero holographic complexity reduces to the sum of two codimension-one contributions and exhibits a universal time evolution, characterized by an exponentially long regime of linear growth followed by saturation at the Heisenberg time. The late-time plateau is a direct result of the spectral level repulsion, a generic feature of quantum chaotic systems, thereby providing a universal mechanism for the saturation of holographic complexity. We also develop a complementary operator interpretation, in which Euclidean wormhole corrections render the semiclassical basis of fixed-complexity states overcomplete. Constructing a complete orthonormal basis then yields well-defined holographic complexity operators that reproduce the same universal time evolution. Our results establish a unified connection among black hole interiors, chaotic spectral statistics, and Euclidean wormholes in quantum gravity.
#3
Critical Ising model as the $M(4,3)$ minimal model of conformal field theory
Dmitry Chelkak, Konstantin Izyurov, Kalle Kytölä 2610.13705v1 [PDF] Score 7
CFTmath-phmath.PR
We construct a linear map from the space of spin polynomials in the critical Ising model on a lattice to the state space of the $M(4,3)$ minimal model of conformal field theory. The kernel of the map consists precisely of null fields, i.e., spin polynomials that vanish in all correlations. For a polynomial $\Op$ which is not a null field, we define its scaling dimension $Δ_{\Op}\in\Z_{\geq0}\cup(\Z_{\geq0}+\frac{1}{8}),$ to be the lowest $\Loper{0}+\Loperbar{0}$ eigenvalue in the decomposition of its image into homogeneous components. We show that as the mesh size $δ$ goes to zero, the rescaled field $δ^{-Δ_{\Op}}\Op$ has a non-trivial scaling limit, when inserted in correlations with other such fields. Moreover, all these limits can be expressed in terms of correlations of primary fields of $M(4,3)$ and the Virasoro action in the continuum. The highest weight vectors of $M(4,3)$, the joint eigenvectors of $\Loper{0}$ and $\Loperbar{0}$ with the same eigenvalues $0,1/16,1/2$ for both, are the images of the constant $1$, the spin at a single lattice vertex, and the energy at a single lattice edge, respectively. For the proof, we construct a map from a larger lattice field space, accommodating also fermion and disorder insertions, to a direct sum of two fermionic Fock spaces. This direct sum contains contains the state spaces of both the $M(4,3)$ and the free fermion CFT. The main technical ingredient of the proof is a new version of the exact fermionic current action on the space of lattice local fields due to C. Hongler, K.K. and F. Viklund, combined with some new advances in discrete complex analysis. Our results are valid on arbitrary isoradial graphs.
#4
Confining Random Potential Quantum Mechanics for Jackiw-Teitelboim Gravity
Dongsu Bak, Chanju Kim, Sang-Heon Yi 2610.13286v1 [PDF] Score 4
Quantum Gravityhep-th
We formulate perturbative Jackiw-Teitelboim gravity as an ensemble of ordinary one-dimensional Hamiltonians with a confining random potential. The leading profile reproduces the disk density of states, while connected cumulants of a single master field encode the topological expansion. After formulating the cumulant power counting required by the topological expansion, we show that the cumulants reproduce all genus-zero multi-boundary amplitudes $\mathcal Z_{0,n}$. At higher genus, we show that a renormalization scheme can be chosen consistently order by order so that imposing one-boundary polynomiality recursively reproduces the perturbative JT string equation and yields the standard JT one-boundary amplitudes $\mathcal Z_{g,1}$. The framework provides a self-contained finite-$S_0$ quantum dynamics with a discrete spectrum and unitary real time evolution, beyond a mere rewriting of the matrix integral. The same confining dynamics also underlies late-time saturation and the dynamical avoidance of the would-be black hole singularity, as shown in our earlier work.
#5
Relaxation Dark Energy: Compositional Dynamics and the Future of Cosmic Acceleration
Gianmassimo Tasinato 2610.13739v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravityastro-ph.COhep-th
If cosmic acceleration is driven by a positive cosmological constant, the universe approaches an eternally accelerating de Sitter state, whose interpretation in quantum gravity remains elusive. We develop a framework in which acceleration is instead a transient stage of dark-sector relaxation towards compositional equilibrium: matter and dark energy approach a stationary density ratio, eventually diluting like pressureless matter. Guided by linear response theory and Onsager's near-equilibrium regression hypothesis, we describe this evolution through a composition variable measuring departure from equilibrium. Extending constant fractional relaxation to the full background evolution yields the dark-energy equation of state $w=w_0a^{-n}$, where $n$ is the relaxation rate per e-fold of expansion. This history connects early dark-energy suppression, an upward phantom crossing and a density maximum to a pressureless scaling future. Using DESI DR2 BAO and Planck distance priors, with and without three alternative supernova compilations, we find a nominal $2.6$--$3.6σ$ preference for nonzero relaxation over constant $w$. The composition framework also motivates a constitutive prescription for linear perturbations that remains regular at phantom crossing. Near equilibrium, regular long-wavelength composition disturbances relax at the background rate to leading order, while small-scale fluctuations propagate as damped sound waves. We prove, however, that for separately conserved matter and dark energy, the pointwise constitutive relation cannot sustain a regular, gradient-stable negative-pressure branch throughout its full past evolution. We explore modifications of the pressure response and their consistency with neighbouring homogeneous histories, identifying requirements for extending the near-equilibrium description to the full cosmological evolution.
#6
G2 resolutions and backtracking in scale-separated AdS$_3$
Aviral Aggarwal, Fien Apers, Fotis Farakos 2610.13586v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFTString Theoryhep-th
We study scale-separated AdS$_3$ models with full moduli stabilization in massive IIA supergravity on G2 orientifolds. In addition to constructing new examples, we investigate the properties of these models once the singularities of the G2 toroidal orbifold are resolved. The blow-up moduli are shown to be stabilized using fluxes in a parametric regime that preserves scale separation and control under the supergravity approximation. We further show that the apparent orientifold intersections in the singular orbifold description do not exist on such resolved spaces. Finally, we analyze the brane dual picture of such models using flux backtracking. We highlight several interesting features from such analysis such as a finite interval solution of the resulting flow equations in some of the models and the desirable low-energy decoupling of brane modes.

Robustness of exact $SU(N)$ Einstein-Skyrme solutions: black holes, wormholes and cosmologies
Carla Henríquez-Baez, Perla Medina, Pablo Rodríguez et al. 2610.13566v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometryhep-thgr-qc
The $SU(2)$ Skyrme model admits exact solutions when coupled to General Relativity in four spacetime dimensions. Here, we demonstrate that relevant gravitational configurations, such as black holes, wormholes, and cosmologies, are robust under the extension to $SU(N)$ with an arbitrary number of flavors. We show that key physical features, such as the thermodynamic properties, the baryon number of the configurations, and the geometry, are crucially determined by the number of flavors as well as the coupling constants of the theory. In particular: first, we prove that an increase in the matter content favors the local thermal stability of spherically symmetric black holes. Second, for cosmological solutions, we demonstrate analytically that as the hadronic matter content increases, the scale factor grows more slowly due to enhanced gravitational attraction, while the expansion rate (measured by the Hubble parameter) increases as a consequence of the higher energy density. Third, we find that the throat of traversable wormhole solutions can be enlarged by increasing the number of flavors, since the energy density required to keep the throat open does not grow indefinitely but rather saturates at a finite value.

Holographic Krylov complexity in confining backgrounds: particle, string, and frames
Jasper Nongmaithem, Deepak Kumar Kar, Ayush Kumar et al. 2610.13555v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFThep-thgr-qchep-ph
We investigate holographic Krylov complexity in bottom-up holographic QCD models describing the confined phase, using the recently proposed correspondence between the growth rate of Krylov complexity and the proper radial momentum of probes, such as particles and strings, falling in the bulk spacetime. As representative confining backgrounds, we consider two horizonless geometries, namely the dilaton-deformed AdS-soliton and the deformed thermal-AdS spacetime, both arising as solutions of an Einstein-dilaton gravity system. We find that the Krylov complexity associated with a massive particle exhibits qualitatively distinct behavior in these two backgrounds when analyzed in the Einstein frame. In the deformed AdS-soliton geometry, the complexity undergoes periodic oscillations, with both the oscillation period and amplitude depending on the parameters of the model. In contrast, in the deformed thermal-AdS geometry, the complexity grows exponentially at late times and does not display oscillatory behavior. We then investigate string dynamics in the string frame and find that the Krylov complexity exhibits oscillatory behavior in both confining backgrounds. This behavior is traced to the emergence of an additional turning point in the deep infrared of the string-frame geometry. Motivated by this observation, we further analyze massive-particle dynamics in the string frame, where we find qualitatively similar behavior in the two confining backgrounds. Our results demonstrate that the Krylov complexity of confined holographic systems depends sensitively on the choice of probe and frame, while also revealing a closer correspondence between the two confining geometries in the string-frame description.

On the quantum statistical relation and the covariant phase space Hamiltonian
José Luis V. Cerdeira, Tomás Ortín 2610.13377v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravityhep-thgr-qc
We show how the covariant phase space Hamiltonian can be constructed in theories in which some of the fields have gauge freedoms. The addition to the action of boundary terms that guarantee the well-posedness of a variational principle and its exact gauge invariance for the prescribed boundary conditions turn out to be essential in this construction. The same ingredients are essential in the use of the action in the framework of Gibbons and Hawking's Euclidean approach to Quantum Gravity and we show how the on-shell action constructed with a Lagrangian with those properties can be expressed in terms of the covariant phase space Hamiltonian. We compute the Hamiltonian of several theories, one of which has Chern--Simons terms, using our prescription and show that, in black-hole backgrounds, the Hamiltonian gives directly the quantum statistical relation including terms related to non-gravitational charges and chemical potentials, as expected.
