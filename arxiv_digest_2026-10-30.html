Are Black Hole and Cosmological Horizon Thermodynamics Necessarily Inequivalent?
Zachary J. Hoelscher 2610.39048v1 [PDF] Score 11
Geometric GravityQuantum GravityString Theoryhep-thastro-ph.COgr-qc
It has recently been argued that cosmological horizon thermodynamics and black hole thermodynamics are generically inequivalent in any consistent, UV complete theory of quantum gravity, where it was suggested that this could be falsified by the identification of a counterexample. This conjecture was motivated in part by noting that cosmological horizons do not incorporate many of the convenient features found in the black holes described by string theory. In this work, we consider as a toy model a universe with a single non-rotating black hole and a positive cosmological constant. We work in the Nariai limit where the mass of the black hole is taken to its maximum possible value, and where we also assert that the quantum field theory living on this background is strictly parity invariant. In the semi-classical regime where we neglect instability of the exact Nariai equilibrium under quantum fluctuations, we find that the observer cannot readily distinguish the physics in the neighborhood of the black hole horizon from the physics in the neighborhood of the cosmological horizon. This does not prove that the two are generically equivalent in any universe, though it does suggest that they cannot be entirely unrelated in general. We propose that any consistent theory of quantum gravity that aims to describe black holes in a universe with a positive cosmological constant should provide a related microscopic origin for the leading semiclassical cosmological entropy and black hole entropy in the neighborhood of the particular limit considered here.

We reconstruct linearized gravitational perturbations in $AdS_{4}$ from a conserved, traceless stress tensor on a fixed conformally flat boundary metric, extending our earlier real Lorentzian construction. Arkady A. Tseytlin 2610.37096v1 [PDF] Score 7
AdS/CFTString Theoryhep-th
We revisit the 2-loop computation of the partition function of the GS superstring expanded near the AdS$_2$ surface in AdS$_5\times S^5$ and AdS$_4\times {\rm CP}^3$, dual to $\frac{1}{2}$ BPS circular Wilson loop in ${\cal N}=4$ SYM and ABJM theories. In arXiv:2601.08809 it was done in AdS$_5\times S^5$ using dimensional regularization and a UV pole was found. Since dimensional regularization is problematic for the GS string, here we use a heat-kernel cutoff instead. Then the contribution of the local path integral measure, proportional to $δ(0)$, can no longer be ignored. We show that it cancels the $δ(0)$ terms coming from the quartic interactions in the action, leaving the square of the difference ${\rm Str}\,E$ between the regularized numbers of bosonic and fermionic modes. The result is thus UV finite, the pole of arXiv:2601.08809 being an artefact of dimensional regularization. If all fields are regularized with the same smearing kernel, ${\rm Str}\,E=0$ and the 2-loop coefficient $f_2$ vanishes, in tension with $f_2=\frac{3}{32π^2}$ in SYM theory. In the AdS$_4\times{\rm CP}^3$ case the same cancellation leaves a finite term proportional to the square of the coincident value of the massless 2d fermion Green's function, giving, ironically, $f_2=\frac{3}{32π^2}$ instead of $f_2=0$ required by localization. This term is the level-zero (string) part of the result of arXiv:2609.14497 for the AdS$_2\times S^1$ M2 brane in AdS$_4\times S^7/\mathbb Z_k$, where it is cancelled by the higher $S^1$ modes, which also remove a finite mass shift of the massless fermion in conflict with the AdS$_2$ supersymmetry. This suggests that the type II string path integral measure should contain an extra contribution, supplied in the IIA case by the parent M2 brane; in the AdS$_5\times S^5$ case it is still to be identified and may resolve the remaining 2-loop mismatch with localization.
