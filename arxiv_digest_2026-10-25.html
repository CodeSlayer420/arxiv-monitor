Quasinormal modes, complex null geodesics and singularity resolution in regular AdS black holes
Heng-Yu Chen, Yasuaki Hikida, Yasutaka Koga 2610.30915v1 [PDF] Score 11
AdS/CFTCFTQuantum Gravityhep-thgr-qc
The black hole singularity is hidden behind its horizon, and it is difficult to determine whether it is resolved by quantum gravity or stringy effects. For an AdS-Schwarzschild black hole, it is known that the two-point function of the dual conformal field theory has a singularity associated with a null geodesic that bounces off the black hole singularity. In this paper, we investigate how the resolution of the black hole singularity is encoded in the dual conformal field theory by considering regular AdS black holes as regularized geometries. Using WKB analysis, we compute the quasinormal modes of regular AdS black holes and read off the dual two-point functions from the modes. We find that the singularity corresponding to the bouncing null geodesic for singular black holes is replaced by a smooth bump associated with a complex, rather than real, null geodesic. The location of the complex geodesic singularity is supported by analyzing the structure of the operator product expansion for the two-point function. Our results provide a holographic signature of black hole singularity resolution and clarify how information about the regular interior is encoded in boundary correlation functions.
#2
Quantum gravity on the bulk cone
Ahmed Almheiri, Matthew Dodelson, Zohar Komargodski 2610.31610v1 [PDF] Score 9
AdS/CFTCFTQuantum Gravityhep-th
Bulk-cone singularities in holographic correlators encode propagation along bulk null geodesics. The prototypical example is an AdS black hole, where incoming null geodesics can wind around the photon sphere and return to the boundary. These singularities appear in real time in the thermal two-point function at finite volume. We show that conformal field theories dual to gravitational theories at finite $G_N$ cannot have such singularities. We rule out these singularities both for individual microstates and for the thermal state. The main thrust of the argument is to invoke an analytic continuation in the temperature. As a microscopic example, we construct an AdS$_3$ geometry with two colliding shocks which exhibits a bulk cone singularity. We show by an explicit calculation in the CFT that this singularity is smoothed out at finite $G_N$. Qualitatively, the singularity resolution is a consequence of the fuzziness of the bulk solution in quantum gravity.

Perturbations and phenomenology of compact objects in quantum gravity theories
Andrea Spina 2610.31536v1 [PDF] Score 7
Quantum Gravitygr-qchep-th
This thesis investigates the perturbative dynamics and phenomenology of compact objects in two approaches to quantum gravity: quadratic gravity and asymptotic safety. Linear perturbations provide a tool to probe the physical properties of spacetimes and their potential observational signatures, including quasinormal modes, black hole shadows, and Hawking radiation. The first part focuses on Einstein-Weyl gravity, where an additional massive spin-2 degree of freedom modifies the dynamics of compact objects. We derive and analyze tensor perturbation for static, spherically symmetric solutions, identifying Gregory-Laflamme-type instabilities in the monopole sector and studying the time domain evolution of higher multipoles. We also investigate universal massive-field tails and the breakdown of the standard eikonal correspondence between quasinormal modes and null geodesics. Particular attention is devoted to veiled singularities, horizonless configurations that satisfy a form of weak cosmic censorship and can mimic black hole shadows. The second part examines regular black holes in asymptotically safe gravity. Using the proper-time flow approach, we construct and investigate a dynamical regular black hole solution arising from gravitational collapse. We analyze its perturbative stability and quasinormal mode spectrum, finding characteristic deviations from Schwarzschild black holes. We explore the phenomenological implications of quantum corrections for shadows and Hawking radiation. While shadow remains compatible with current constraints, the modified evaporation process features lower temperatures and suppressed emission rates, with implications for primordial black holes and their possible contribution to dark matter. Overall, this work demonstrates how the perturbative dynamics of compact objects can connect quantum gravity models to potentially observable astrophysical phenomena.

Monodromy Defects of 3D Wilson-Fisher Criticality on the Fuzzy Sphere
Yin Tang, W. Zhu 2610.31269v1 [PDF] Score 7
CFThep-thcond-mat.stat-mechcond-mat.str-el
Monodromy (twist) line defects arise as the boundaries of topological symmetry surfaces, which are among the simplest non-trivial conformal defects with direct realizations as magnetic vortex in quantum magnets, as Wilson and t Hooft lines of gauge theories, and as the replica twist operators in quantum information theory. However, their interacting dynamics remains difficult to characterize non-perturbatively. Here we design a way to realize the monodromy boundary conditions in the fuzzy-sphere regularization by threading a symmetry-selective Aharonov-Bohm flux through the two poles of the sphere. We apply this construction to the $\mathbb{Z}_2$ twist of the 3D Ising conformal field theory (CFT), $π$-twist of the 3D O(2) CFT and three inequivalent monodromies of O(3) (centre twist, z-axis $π$-twist and the reflection). For each defect, we extract the low-lying defect spectrum together with bulk-to-defect and defect-defect operator product expansion coefficients, which characterize the operators living on the defect line, their mutual interactions, and how they couple to the surrounding critical bulk. Among the low-lying defect primaries resolved in our spectra, we find no relevant scalar perturbation that preserves all the symmetries of the corresponding defect. The relevant deformations identified in our calculations break at least one preserved symmetry, providing evidence that these monodromy defects are stable against symmetry-preserving relevant perturbations. These results establish a systematic set of non-perturbative conformal data for monodromy defects beyond the Ising twist. Our construction therefore turns the symmetry flux into a tunable knob of the fuzzy-sphere regularization, opening a route to exploring the response of critical matter to distinct defects.

The Spectral Edge at Large Central Charge and Black Hole Statistics
Eric Perlmutter 2610.31413v1 [PDF] Score 6
AdS/CFTCFThep-th
We introduce a new approach to high-energy spectra in AdS$_3$/CFT$_2$, addressing the open challenge of incorporating spectral statistics into the modular bootstrap. Using the analytic theory of $L$-functions attached to two-dimensional CFTs, we leverage modular constraints among high-energy states to derive a sum rule relating the spectral edge to bulk spectral fluctuations. As an application, we establish a refined modular bootstrap bound: letting $Δ_1$ be the conformal dimension of the lightest excited Virasoro primary of a unitary CFT of central charge $c$, we show that $Δ_1 - {c-1\over 12} \leq o(c)$, with at most one exceptional value of the scalar gap at each sufficiently large $c$; moreover, along any sequence in $c$ for which such exceptional gaps are realized, the remaining gaps obey the stronger bound $Δ_1 - {c-1\over 12} \leq O(1)$. The result applies to theories with logarithmic rigidity of spectral counting fluctuations, as in random matrix theory.

On the direct SLE-martingale approach to the integrability of Conformal Loop Ensembles
Jason Miller, Wendelin Werner 2610.31648v1 [PDF] Score 6
CFTQuantum Gravitymath.PRmath-ph
This paper is about the derivation of "integrability results" (i.e., for example, explicit formulas for the probability of events involving a few marked points) for Conformal Loop Ensembles, that are the (conjectured) scaling limits of discrete crititical planar lattice models from Statistical Physics. We discuss the explicit solutions to the PDEs provided by the original SLE/CLE martingale approach that AI now makes it possible to uncover. This provides short and direct new proofs of a number of formulas that have recently been derived based on Liouville quantum gravity, conformal field theory or Coulomb gas methods, and also makes it possible to derive some new ones. We briefly illustrate this with the examples of the backbone exponent and annulus crossing probabilities for critical percolation, but this direct approach seems to apply to a wide class of observables of two-dimensional systems related to conformal field theories.

String Amplitudes on Integer Rectangles
Song He, Yong Zhang, Fan Zhu et al. 2610.31197v1 [PDF] Score 4
CFTString Theoryhep-th
Hidden zeros of tree-level string amplitudes of colored scalars lie on loci specified by integer-valued dimensionless momentum contractions arranged in rectangles on the kinematic mesh. We study the same kinematic loci for open-string amplitudes with gluons, fermions and higher-spin states at arbitrary finite mass levels. Disk monodromy relations yield $\textit{zero-locus factorization}$: at regular points where the monodromy system is invertible, the amplitude is a weighted sum of physical three-point amplitudes sewn to complete on-shell $(n-1)$-point amplitudes. Hidden zeros follow when the external wavefunctions annihilate all contributing three-point couplings. In the low-energy limit, the massless zero-integer branch recovers the corresponding field-theory factorization. For closed strings, including gravitons, persistent cubic zeros in both chiral sectors remove the disk poles, allowing the Kawai--Lewellen--Tye momentum-kernel zero to imply amplitude zeros. The operator product expansions of external vertices give a common worldsheet origin for these conditions across spins and mass levels.

Testing charged Simpson-Visser-AdS spacetimes with twin-peak QPOs
Saeed Ullah Khan, Javlon Rayimbaev, Muhammad Zahid et al. 2610.31534v1 [PDF] Score 4
AdS/CFTgr-qchep-ph
In this manuscript, we study circular geodesics and twin-peak high-frequency quasi-periodic oscillations (HF-QPOs) in the charged Simpson--Visser--anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime, which depends on a charge parameter $Q$, a regularization scale $b$ and an AdS radius $l$. Written in terms of the areal radius $R=\sqrt{r^{2}+b^{2}}$, the specific energy, angular momentum, Keplerian frequency and innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) of test particles coincide with those of the Reissner--Nordström--AdS black hole, so $b$ enters the epicyclic spectrum only through a factor $1-b^{2}/R^{2}$ in the radial frequency. We give this frequency and the ISCO condition in closed form. We then fit the relativistic-precession model to the HF-QPO pairs of GRO~J1655$-$40 and XTE~J1550$-$564, with their dynamical masses included as Gaussian priors. The Schwarzschild limit gives $χ^{2}=32.9$ for two degrees of freedom. One additional deformation removes this tension: $Q/M=0.814^{+0.059}_{-0.074}$ at fixed $l=100M$, or $l=40.6^{+6.2}_{-4.8}\,M$ at $Q=0$, both with $χ^{2}\simeq0.13$. These two solutions lie on a single degeneracy valley in the $(Q,l)$ plane, and, at $l=100M$, $b$ is left unconstrained up to $b\simeq3.9M$ (68\%), a limit set by the charge prior rather than by the data. A Kerr black hole with spin $a\simeq0.3$--$0.4$ reproduces the same pairs, so the inferred deformation most likely absorbs the spin neglected by the static metric. Our results therefore describe degeneracies among orbital-frequency deformations; they are not a measurement of an electric charge or of a cosmological constant.

G. Bonelli, S. Lacroix, T. Pedroni et al. 2610.31672v1 [PDF] Score 3

We construct a family of integrable 2d sigma models through 4d Chern-Simons theory and show that their classical point-particle reduction corresponds to a Hitchin system. The integrable Hitchin quantisation then provides a natural candidate Hamiltonian for the one-loop reduced dynamics of the sigma models, which coincides with the Perelman operator in all cases examined. Half-Completeness from Standard Gravitational Dispersion Relations
Lukas W. Lindwasser, Laurentiu Rodina, Justinas Rumbutis 2610.31665v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravityhep-th
The completeness hypothesis states that quantum gravity realizes every representation allowed by its gauge group. Previous S-matrix arguments used the graviton pole to constrain exchanged representations, including a proof of completeness by imposing an additional high-energy bound on crossing-odd amplitudes. We investigate what follows without this assumption, using standard twice-subtracted dispersion relations, positivity, and crossing symmetry. For external massless SU(2) isospin $J$, we prove ``half-completeness'': either massive exchanges include every isospin $j\le J$, or at least one $j>J$ is required. At $J=1$, we construct an explicit amplitude with only $j=0,1$ exchanges that satisfies these conditions but violates the crossing-odd bound. This establishes that the additional high-energy assumption does not follow from the standard framework. For $J\ge2$, however, we show that lower-only spectra cannot be realized within a class of string-inspired Euler amplitudes, leaving their existence an open question. More generally, for scalar matter transforming under any compact internal symmetry group, we prove that massive singlets of unbounded Lorentz spin must be exchanged.

High precision black hole scattering
Benjamin Sauer 2610.31238v1 [PDF] Score 3
String Theoryhep-thgr-qc
In this thesis we compute the impulse of a classical black hole scattering encounter in the Post-Minkowskian (PM) expansion - a weak-field expansion in Newton's constant $G$ - up to the fifth order. We describe the black holes as point particles within an effective field theory framework and couple them through gravity. The classical equations of motion are solved diagrammatically using the worldline quantum field theory (WQFT) formalism, in which the solution with causal boundary conditions is encoded in tree-level Feynman diagrams built from graviton and worldline fields. The Feynman integrals arising at each order are reduced by advanced IBP reduction techniques and evaluated with the method of differential equations. We find that, in the solution of these integrals, periods over Calabi--Yau manifolds - familiar from string theory - appear and contribute to the classical impulse at the fifth Post-Minkowskian order. The results of this work are the first analytic results for the scattering observables at fifth Post-Minkowskian order, together with the appearance of Calabi--Yau threefold periods in a classical physics observable.

Timelike Supersymmetric Geometries in Seven-Dimensional Supergravity
Michèle Levi 2610.31191v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFTString Theoryhep-thmath-phmath.DG
Seven-dimensional supergravity poses a uniquely intricate supersymmetry problem, combining topologically massive higher-form gauge dynamics, distinct Clifford algebra properties, and rich geometric structures. We first establish the complete formulation of the maximal Cartan-gauged theory in terms of the gauge-invariant higher-form flux, including both the bosonic action and supersymmetry variations. This formulation, which also makes the smooth ungauged limit manifest, is achieved by requiring a consistent truncation to the minimal Cartan-gauged theory in the equal-charge limit. We construct the complete bilinear system for a timelike Killing vector, where additional internal orientation of the symplectic-Majorana spinor arises already in the scalar sector, uniquely among pure minimal ungauged theories. In the equal-charge sector, we decouple the algebraic constraints into SU$(3)$-isotypic components, and analyse the differential bilinear relations, determining the permitted dilaton gradient, fluxes, fibration twist and intrinsic torsion of the base. We prove that with gauging and active Cartan flux, the complex scalar bilinear vanishes identically. We also construct a Killing gauge for the higher-form potential compatible with the self-duality condition. We show that with gauging but no active Cartan flux, the Killing vector also acts as a generator of the gauged R-symmetry, and the base structure is enlarged from SU$(3)$ to U$(3)$. We find that BPS-flow solutions realize this setting. With supersymmetry, we isolate a single independent component of the 3-form equation across an exhaustive four-class partition for both theories within this setup. Applied to known and proposed solutions in both theories, this classification exposes unexplored sectors and provides an accessible toolbox for constructing and testing solutions and developing new geometric and holographic applications.
