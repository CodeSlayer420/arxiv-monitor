Holographic Tessellations Tensor Networks $\{p,q\}$ from Entangled Quantum Tetrahedron in Loop Quantum Gravity
Deden M. Akbar, Freddy P. Zen, Jusak S. Kosasih 2611.25993v1 [PDF] Score 18
AdS/CFTEntanglement & GeometryQuantum Gravityhep-th
The relationship between spin network states in loop quantum gravity and bulk--boundary duality is further explored using the tessellated holographic tensor network. We develop a holographic tensor network construction based on quantum entanglement between tetrahedra in loop quantum gravity (LQG). This construction relates four-valent intertwiner spaces and Bell states dressed with $SU(2)$ holonomies to effective tensors on hyperbolic $\{p,q\}$ tessellations. For the $\{5,4\}$ tessellation, six tetrahedra are assembled into a single effective cell with five network legs and one logical index. Provided that the microscopic transfer map has full row rank, a right inverse and a completion on the kernel space are employed to construct an isometric embedding, yielding an effective tensor proportional to a perfect tensor. The construction is generalized to the $p=2n-1$ family, with local dimensions supporting absolutely maximally entangled states. On finite networks satisfying a radial orientation condition, an appropriate normalization produces an isometric bulk--boundary map. For cuts satisfying the isometry condition on both sides, the boundary entropy decomposes into a link contribution $\sum_{\ell}\log(2j_{\ell}+1)$ and the bulk entropy, thereby providing a discrete realization of the quantum Ryu--Takayanagi structure. Applications to the black hole interior are formulated via postselection-based non-isometric encoding. Assuming additional Haar statistics, this framework is used to analyze the preservation of overlaps on average and the competition between entropy branches underlying the Page Curve. These results establish a constructive link between the geometric degrees of freedom of LQG and holographic codes, while clarifying the conditions required to connect them to geometric area in LQG, the location of the quantum extremal surface, and evaporation of black hole.

Fast extraction of universal data from temporal spectrum: Applications to Haagerup CFT and monopole scattering
Tokiro Numasawa, Boris De Vos, Masataka Watanabe et al. 2611.26257v1 [PDF] Score 7
CFTcond-mat.str-elhep-lathep-th
Extracting precise universal data on conformal field theory (CFT) on the lattice traditionally requires large-scale simulations of the ground states and excitations of critical systems. Here, we propose an alternative accurate and fast algorithm, where we read off the CFT spectrum from the transfer matrix spectrum of an infinite matrix product state after a short imaginary-time evolution. We apply this method to extract the charge-resolved spectrum of a Haagerup CFT to higher levels and obtain a central charge $c = 2.137(27)$. Moreover, we show that the Maldacena--Ludwig monopole boundary can be realized as the ground state of the Fidkowski--Kitaev Hamiltonian after space-time rotation.

Classification of Chern-Simons Couplings and Deformed Pull-Backs on D-Branes
Armin Hatefi, Ehsan Hatefi 2611.26374v1 [PDF] Score 3
String Theoryhep-thgr-qchep-ph
We establish a framework for classifying higher-derivative Ramond-Ramond (RR) couplings on Dp-branes, focusing on the complete scattering amplitude of a closed-string RR state, a gauge field, and two transverse scalars. The low-energy expansion reveals infinite massless poles and local interaction terms, surpassing standard Myers and Taylor expansions. We propose a higher-class deformation of the pullback using mixed Chern-Simons transgression operators to address these complexities. Our three conjectures are as follows: 1. For fixed field content and derivative order, effective couplings form a finite-dimensional space under stated equivalences. 2. A family of mixed RR, gauge, and scalar interactions exists at all orders, represented by a finite number of effective even forms. 3. The higher-derivative contact-term sector can be constructed from a finite set of basic tensor structures using polynomials in derivative symbols. We introduce a higher-class pullback operator \( P_{\infty} \) and mixed transgression operators \( \mathcal{T}_{\infty} \), emphasizing amplitude-derived pole residues and local contact terms as key physical data. This formalism distinguishes admissible tensor structures, presenting characteristic-form aspects as conjectures alongside their validity considerations.

Black holes and 2-group symmetry from blackfolds in minimal supergravity
Jay Armas, Giorgos Batzios, Pedro Vicente Marto et al. 2611.26287v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFTString Theoryhep-thgr-qc
We investigate the black hole phase diagram of five-dimensional minimal supergravity using the blackfold approach and the framework of generalized symmetries. Starting from supergravity coupled to probe sources, we derive the 2-group (non-)conservation laws, construct the equilibrium action for a two-dimensional 2-group fluid, and compute the associated global conserved charges. We then match the thermodynamics of the general non-extremal black string in the ungauged theory to that of this fluid, organizing the blackfold effective theory and its solutions in terms of symmetry invariants. In the non-extremal ungauged sector, we perturbatively construct new black rings, Saturns, di-rings, and bi-rings, including a black Saturn with seven independent parameters. Taking appropriate extremal limits, we identify several extremal branches, some previously unexplored and others anticipated, as well as BPS branches that approximate known supersymmetric families. Finally, we apply the same framework to construct extremal black rings in AdS$_5$, identifying three regular branches that are promising candidates for an exact completion.

Worldsheet Effective Theory of Abelian-Higgs Cosmic Strings beyond Nambu--Goto
Sergio Alameda-Calvo, Josu C. Aurrekoetxea, Jose J. Blanco-Pillado et al. 2611.26013v1 [PDF] Score 3
String Theoryhep-thastro-ph.COhep-ph
Cosmic strings can support long-lived excitations confined to their cores, opening channels for energy exchange with the transverse Goldstone modes that describe the string's motion. We derive a covariant worldsheet theory of their coupled dynamics directly from the Abelian-Higgs model at critical coupling, with all interaction coefficients fixed by the microscopic theory. The resulting action, which we validate against numerical lattice field theory simulations, extends the Nambu--Goto description to include a massive scalar shape mode and a massive vector mode. Dualizing the vector reveals a geometric distinction: the shape scalar mode couples to intrinsic worldsheet curvature, while the pseudoscalar couples to normal bundle curvature. These interactions explain the distinct parametric instabilities observed in lattice simulations and predict how the relative polarization of colliding transverse waves controls the excitation of the two internal modes. Our results connect microscopic string structure to the nonlinear worldsheet dynamics, providing a predictive framework for cosmic strings beyond the Nambu--Goto approximation.

Tunable Ground-State Entanglement in Fully Connected Quantum Systems: From Logarithmic to Volume-Law Scaling
Donghoon Kim, Tomotaka Kuwahara 2611.26430v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & Geometryquant-phcond-mat.dis-nncond-mat.stat-mech
Entanglement scaling is a central probe of the complexity of quantum many-body ground states, yet once geometric locality is absent, it can depend sensitively on the structure and normalization of interactions. For fully connected Hamiltonians with the natural Kac normalization, nondegenerate gapped ground states are known to obey a logarithmic entanglement bound. Here we ask how the ground-state entanglement changes when this interaction normalization is continuously relaxed away from the Kac scale. We answer this question by constructing an explicit all-to-all bipartite Hamiltonian of $n$ qubits with Fourier pairing amplitudes and pairwise interaction strength proportional to $n^{-κ}$. For sufficiently small fixed coupling, we prove that every pure ground state has entanglement entropy $Θ(n^{2-2κ} \log n)$ for $1/2<κ\leq1$ and $Θ(n)$ at $κ=1/2$. We further show that the ground state is unique and separated from the rest of the spectrum by a system-size-independent gap for $3/4 \leq κ\leq 1$, while weak-coupling analysis and numerical calculations suggest that the gap remains open also for $1/2 \leq κ< 3/4$. Thus, varying only the interaction normalization yields a controlled hierarchy from logarithmic scaling at the natural Kac normalization $κ=1$ to increasingly strong subextensive entanglement and ultimately to a volume law, showing that even an arbitrarily weak relaxation of this normalization precludes a universal logarithmic entanglement bound.
