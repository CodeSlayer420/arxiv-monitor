Entanglement island and Page curve for one-sided rotating Kerr black holes
Soumen Pari 2611.32438v1 [PDF] Score 15
Entanglement & GeometryGeometric GravityQuantum Gravityhep-thgr-qc
We investigate the information paradox for one-sided asymptotically flat rotating Kerr black holes formed through gravitational collapse using the island prescription. Near the horizon, the dynamics of a scalar field can be described by an effective two-dimensional theory, which provides the framework for our analysis. We study how rotation affects the location of the island boundary $\partial I$, the Page time, and the scrambling time, treating non-extremal and extremal Kerr black holes separately. For non-extremal black holes, the emergence of an island prevents the unbounded growth of Hawking radiation entropy at late times and restores the unitary Page curve. We further find that the island boundary depends on the location of the cutoff surface, in contrast to the behaviour found for eternal Kerr black holes. In the extremal case, the late-time entanglement entropy of Hawking radiation approaches the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of the extremal black hole. Our results demonstrate the important role of black hole geometry in determining the behaviour of entanglement entropy and suggest that the island prescription provides a semiclassical resolution of the information paradox for one-sided rotating Kerr black holes.

Logarithmic soft theorems in QED and gravity can be understood as conservation laws associated with asymptotic symmetries. We compute vacuum tripartite Rényi multi-entropy in a two-dimensional compact-boson conformal field theory (CFT). In the covariant phase space of the near-horizon Maxwell field, the soft charge is a corner term. The memory is the zero mode of the gauge-invariant radiative field along the generators. It is conjugate to the boundary gauge mode that labels the degenerate horizon vacua, and Gauss' law fixes it by the flux of charge through the horizon. The Noether flux of the residual gauge transformation through the stretched horizon is not conserved by the scattering, because charged matter crosses the stretched horizon in both directions. The flux with its matter part removed is the soft charge, which generates the large gauge transformation. The endpoint data on the two branches give two boundary observables, the time-integrated transverse magnetic and electric fields. The field radiated by a scattering process contributes only to the first, where it cancels the change in the Coulomb memory of the charges, so that the soft charge is conserved. A charge that crosses the horizon enters both. We also discuss the infrared divergence, the dressing of the external states, and the fate of the memory in the Hartle--Hawking state, where it performs a random walk driven by the Hawking radiation.

Quantum Gravityhep-th
It has been emphasized recently that some extremal black holes are "critical" solutions, lying at the threshold between gravitational collapse and dispersion. We study the effect of quantum gravity on critical solutions for the collapse of a thin shell of charged dust. We consider the Hamiltonian that generates the shell equation of motion with respect to the exterior Killing time and coincides with the ADM energy on-shell. Near criticality, this Hamiltonian is shown to be canonically equivalent to that of a Liouville theory with negative potential. After quantization, this model predicts that a collapsing shell has a non-trivial probability of undergoing a quantum mechanical bounce when its energy above extremality is comparable to the "breakdown scale". The bounce probability approaches unity at extremality. Hence this model predicts that the critical surface in the classical theory is thickened quantum mechanically into a "critical layer". The probability density function for the black hole energy is suppressed below the breakdown scale in the same way as found recently by Turiaci and Wu.

The Cosmological Constant as a Clock and the Vilenkin Wavefunction as a Resonance
Stephon Alexander, Marcell Howard 2611.32987v1 [PDF] Score 3
Quantum Gravitygr-qchep-th
We address the problem of time in quantum gravity using a covariant formulation of unimodular gravity. As a result, the cosmological constant is canonically conjugate to a global four-volume variable, which serves as a clock and leads to a time-dependent Wheeler--DeWitt equation. We construct exact, background-independent solutions related to the full Chern--Simons--Kodama state and demonstrate unitary evolution in minisuperspace. The Chern--Simons functional also provides an intrinsic gravitational clock. These solutions reveal that the Vilenkin wavefunction is a gravitational Gamow resonance, in which its non-normalizability follows from the outgoing boundary condition. The tunneling probability into the expanding branch increases with the positive cosmological constant. These results give a dynamical interpretation of the wavefunction of the universe and clarify the role of its boundary conditions.

Holographic Spread Complexity of a Probe Particle with General Initial Data
Zekun Qian, Zhehan Li, Jia Tian 2611.32427v1 [PDF] Score 3
AdS/CFThep-th
We investigate the holographic spread complexity of a massive particle probing global AdS$_3$ with general initial data. By constructing an adapted Hamiltonian, we map each bulk geodesic to a boundary Krylov dynamical pair, whose dynamics are governed solely by the conserved chiral charges. We find that, for generic initial data, the original identification $\dot{C}\sim p_ρ$ fails; instead, both the complexity and its growth rate depend nonlinearly on the bulk Noether charges. We systematically analyze this nonlinear encoding via several perturbative expansions. We further investigate two refined variants: the symmetry-resolved spread complexity, which restores the bulk radial $π$-periodicity, and the chiral spread complexity, which admits an exact closed form approaching the standard complexity at small angular momentum. Finally, by directly quantizing an effective AdS$_2$ theory, we obtain a new holographic interpretation of spread complexity in terms of the lapse function.

Modular fluctuation relations on local causal horizons
Marcos L. W. Basso 2611.33384v1 [PDF] Score 3
Entanglement & GeometryGeometric Gravitygr-qcquant-ph
Local causal horizons provide a setting for studying horizon thermodynamics in the neighborhood of any regular spacetime point. Here, we investigate this description beyond mean energy and entropy balances by studying fluctuations of boost-energy transfer induced by unitary perturbations of the horizon reference state. Using the modular structure of the horizon algebra and a thermal reference state, we construct an energy transfer probability measure and derive integral and detailed fluctuation relations with the mean entropy production being expressed in terms of the Araki relative entropy. The formulation accommodates type-III algebras without introducing a reduced density matrix and can be extended to global bifurcate Killing horizons admitting the required thermal reference state. For coherent scalar-field perturbations, we obtain a closed form for the characteristic function, identify the mean transfer with the excess boost-energy flux, and derive a thermodynamic uncertainty relation bounding the variance in terms of the mean transfer. We then explore the geometric implications by postulating a stochastic horizon entropy--area correspondence that associates an area change with each counted energy transfer. Under this additional hypothesis, we obtain fluctuation relations for area changes and an area change uncertainty bound for coherent perturbations.
